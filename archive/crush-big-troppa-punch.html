Crush Back to the 90s Big Troppa Punch

The soda that sells our decade back to us.

Still sold!
MakerCrush
YearsLimited edition · the decade as a flavor
StatusLimited edition
ShelfDrinks

Read the can slowly, because the whole joke — and it is a good one — is printed right on it. "Limited." "Back to the 90s." "Big Troppa Punch." Crush has released a tropical punch soda named in a graffiti scrawl, colored like a windbreaker, and marketed not as a flavor but as a *decade*. Our decade. The one this entire publication exists to archive is now a limited-edition soda flavor, and honestly? We could not be prouder, or weirder about it, if we tried.

Read the original post on Substack

The story

This is the first time a product has arrived at www.90sfoodie.com that is *about* this publication's subject the way a tribute band is about a band. Big Troppa Punch is not a 90s soda. It is a soda *in 90s cosplay* — and the fascinating question is not whether the cosplay is accurate. It is what it means that "the 90s" has become a flavor at all.

A Name Built Out of Pure 1995

Start with the name, because the name is doing three jobs at once. "Big Troppa" is a wink at "Big Poppa," the Notorious B.I.G.'s 1994 anthem — the kind of reference that works as a homophone joke for adults and as pure swagger for everyone else. Then "Back to the 90s" is written in marker strokes and cartoon bubbles, with a halo doodled over the lettering like a kid tagging a notebook. The strawberry on the can is drawn fat and red, less like fruit than like a sticker from a Trapper Keeper. Every element is quotation: hip-hop, graffiti, school supplies, snack-bar signage.

And Crush is the perfect brand to attempt it, because Crush spent the actual 90s as one of the soda aisle's great flavor maximalists — the orange that outsold your expectations, the strawberry, the grape, the pineapple, a whole fruit stand carbonated. A "Back to the 90s" line from Crush is not a stretch. It is a home game. The tropical punch inside the can — bright, sweet, defiantly artificial in the honorable way — is almost beside the point, and the can knows it: the flavor is called *punch*, the most party-forward word in the soda vocabulary, and the color of the bottle version is the exact pink of a decade's worth of after-school specials.

Nostalgia, Bottled at Industrial Scale

Let us be clear-eyed about what we are looking at, because the archive at www.90sfoodie.com has watched this movie from the other side for years. For most of this publication's life, the arrangement was simple: the 90s products were gone, and the fans kept the memory warm for free. Petitions circulated. Photos got shared. Demand accumulated in public until a company decided the math worked. That is how Surge returned, how Ecto Cooler keeps returning, how French Toast Crunch came back.

Big Troppa Punch represents the next stage: the companies have stopped waiting for a specific product to be demanded. They are now selling the *era itself*, pre-packaged, with a new flavor inside it that nobody has memories of — because nobody needs to. The memories attach to the frame, not the drink. You are not buying a soda you loved in 1996. You are buying 1996, drinkable, with a soda inside.

Is that cynical? A little. Is it also, frankly, delightful? Completely. The can is genuinely fun to look at. The pink is the correct pink. The graffiti is the correct graffiti. Somebody's design team clearly loved the assignment, and the whole object treats the decade the way this publication does: as a place with a look, a sound, and a flavor profile of its own — loud, sweet, and not remotely interested in being subtle. When readers send finds like this to www.90sfoodie.com, the tone is never outrage. It is recognition. *They are selling our childhood at the gas station now, and we are buying two.*

What "90s Flavor" Actually Means

Here is the part worth slowing down for. If "the 90s" is a flavor, what is the recipe? Big Troppa Punch's answer — tropical punch, turned up, dyed pink — is one defensible draft. The decade's soda aisle ran on fruit punches and neon: flavors named for colors, colors named for attitudes. But talk to ten 90s kids and you will get ten recipes, because a decade does not taste like one thing. It tastes like everything you were allowed to want before you had to read a nutrition label.

That is why this can belongs in the archive even though it is brand new. It is evidence — primary-source evidence — of what the 90s have become in the culture: not a set of products, but an aesthetic with purchasing power, strong enough to launch flavors that never existed. The products in our archive spent thirty years becoming valuable as memories. Big Troppa Punch is what that value looks like when it comes back over the counter wearing a price tag. The tribute act only exists because the original band still sells out the arena in people's heads.

There is also a quieter compliment buried in the can. Crush did not call it "retro" or "throwback" or "vintage." It said *Back to the 90s*, like it is a place you can simply go — and the entire readership of this publication has been saying exactly that, in exactly those words, for years. We did not just live there. We kept the porch light on.

The Verdict From the Archive

So: is Big Troppa Punch any good, as a soda? It is a sweet tropical punch that knows its job is joy, and it does the job with its chest out. But grading the liquid misses the artifact. This can is a milestone marker on the road this whole archive has been documenting — the moment the nostalgia economy graduated from reviving products to bottling the decade itself. First they brought back our sodas. Now they are brewing new ones in our name.

Whether that thrills you or worries you probably depends on how you feel about tribute bands. Our position, for the record, at www.90sfoodie.com: the tribute exists because the originals mattered, and as long as companies keep having to come *back to the 90s* to find their energy, the decade's title is not really in dispute. The 90s are not a trend. Trends do not get thirty years of encores.
