Butterfinger Ice Cream Nuggets

Twenty poppable ice cream balls in a chocolate Butterfinger shell — sold by the tub.

Gone, not forgotten
MakerNestlé
YearsEarly 1990s · gone by the late 1990s
StatusDiscontinued
ShelfFrozen Treats

Picture a summer night in the mid 1990s. The freezer doors fog as you slide one open, and there, between the Push-Ups and the Flintstones Push Pops, sits a squat tub with a white lid and an orange label: Butterfinger Ice Cream Nuggets. Twenty balls of vanilla ice cream wrapped in chocolate and that crispety, crunchety, peanut-buttery Butterfinger crunch. You did not need a stick. You did not need a bowl. You needed a spoon at most — honestly, most of us just used our fingers.

The story

If you remember them, you are already tasting them. If you have never heard of them, welcome to one of the most missed discontinued snacks of the decade — a lost 90s ice cream treat that fans search for, post about, and beg Nestle to bring back.

What exactly were Butterfinger Ice Cream Nuggets? They arrived in the early 1990s, when candy bars were invading the freezer aisle and every brand wanted an ice cream twin. Each nugget was a poppable sphere of smooth vanilla ice cream coated in a milk chocolate shell loaded with Butterfinger pieces. The shell shattered with that flaky crunch, the peanut butter flavor hit, and the cold vanilla smoothed everything out. Two bites, maybe three, and it was gone — which is why nobody in history ate just one.

They came in a resealable tub of twenty, and that tub mattered. Ice cream novelties in the 90s were built for one kid, one serving, one hot afternoon. The Nuggets tub turned Butterfinger into a shareable freezer snack years before shareable was a marketing word. It lived on the top shelf of the family freezer, and every trip past the kitchen became a negotiation.

The Nuggets also landed at the peak of Butterfinger mania. Bart Simpson was on television warning the world that nobody better lay a finger on his Butterfinger. Butterfinger BB's — the little candy balls in the yellow bag — were a movie theater staple. The candy itself was everywhere, and the ice cream nuggets felt like its final form: the crunch everyone loved, finally served cold.

So why did they disappear? Nobody at Nestle ever held a press conference about it. The freezer aisle of the 1990s was an unforgiving place, crowded with novelty ice creams competing for the same few doors, and products vanished when the math stopped working. By the end of the decade, Butterfinger Ice Cream Nuggets were gone, and unlike the bar, the BB's, or the later Butterfinger Bites, they never came back. Butterfinger ice cream survives today in other shapes, but the tub of twenty poppable nuggets remains a ghost.
