Pepsi Blue

The electric blue berry soda that defined 2002 — and vanished.

Gone, not forgotten
MakerPepsiCo
Years2002–2004 · brief return 2021
StatusDiscontinued
ShelfDrinks

In the summer of 2002, Pepsi did something no major cola had dared to do: it turned blue. Not "blue raspberry candy" blue. Not "sports drink" blue. A deep, electric, almost glowing blue that looked like it had been beamed down from another planet.

The story

Pepsi Blue didn't look like soda. It looked like the future. And for one bright summer, it was.

Pepsi Blue was a berry-flavored cola launched by PepsiCo in mid-2002. The flavor was officially described as "berry," though ask anyone who drank it and you'll get a dozen different answers — blueberry, raspberry, "like blue candy melted into cola." The taste was sweet, fruity, and unmistakably not regular Pepsi.

The color was the whole point. This was the era of extreme everything — extreme sports, extreme snacks, extreme sodas. Clear cola (Crystal Pepsi) had been the 90s experiment; blue cola was the 2000s answer. If Crystal Pepsi asked "what if cola were clear?", Pepsi Blue asked "what if cola looked radioactive?"

And kids in 2002 were all in. The bottles — especially the glass ones sold internationally, like the pair in the photo above — looked incredible lined up in a fridge. It was a soda you bought as much for the look as the taste.

Pepsi didn't do subtle in 2002. Pepsi Blue launched with a massive summer campaign, heavy TV rotation, and tie-ins that planted it firmly in pop culture. It was positioned as the drink of the moment — youthful, bold, a little rebellious.

For a few months, it worked. Pepsi Blue was everywhere: convenience stores, vending machines, lunch tables. It became a genuine cultural artifact of the summer of 2002, the kind of product that now lives in "remember this?" compilations.

By 2004, Pepsi Blue was gone from US and Canadian shelves — just two years after launch. The official reasons were never dramatic: sales cooled after the novelty wore off, and Pepsi shifted focus to other products.

But the legend only grew. Discontinued sodas have a way of becoming more beloved in absence. Pepsi Blue joined the pantheon of gone-too-soon drinks, right alongside Crystal Pepsi and Surge — the holy trinity of "bring it back" soda nostalgia.

In the summer of 2021 — nearly two decades later — Pepsi brought it back for a limited run. The internet lost its mind. Nostalgia YouTubers filmed taste tests. Millennials who'd been kids in 2002 bought cases.

The comeback proved what fans had been saying all along: Pepsi Blue wasn't just a gimmick. It was a genuinely beloved flavor tied to a specific, golden summer. The limited run sold out fast, and then — as quickly as it returned — it was gone again.

Pepsi Blue hit at the exact intersection of flavor, color, and cultural moment. It was a soda that understood its assignment: be bold, be weird, be unforgettable.

Every few years, the rumors start again. Is Pepsi Blue coming back for good? So far, the answer has always been a limited run and a vanishing act. But the demand never fades — because some drinks aren't just drinks. They're time machines.

Did you drink Pepsi Blue in 2002? Did you grab the 2021 comeback? Tell me in the comments — I want to know where you land on the great berry-flavor debate.
