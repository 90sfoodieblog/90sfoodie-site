Orbitz Soda

The soda with floating orbs — a lava lamp you could drink.

Gone, not forgotten
MakerClearly Canadian
Years1997–1998
StatusDiscontinued
ShelfDrinks

If you were around in the late 1990s, you probably remember a quirky, boundary-pushing drink that was hard to ignore. Orbitz soda was a delightful oddity — a vibrant, bubbly concoction that seemed to spring straight from the imagination of a bold and adventurous decade. It was a drink that didn't just satisfy your thirst but also offered an entirely new experience for your senses. Orbitz wasn't just soda; it was an event.

Read the original post on Substack

The story

Debuting in 1997, Orbitz was introduced by Clearly Canadian, a company known for its sparkling waters. At a time when the beverage market was saturated with sugary sodas and flavored waters, Orbitz came on the scene like a burst of confetti. The drink featured a clear, carbonated liquid with colorful, gel-like orbs suspended throughout. These weren't just any orbs — they were the star of the show, turning each bottle into a visually captivating, almost hypnotic display.

The genius behind Orbitz wasn't just in the product itself but also in its boldness. It was an era of novelty and new experiences. The '90s were all about pushing boundaries, and Orbitz did exactly that. People weren't just drinking soda anymore — they were enjoying a multi-sensory experience that made every sip feel like an adventure. It was like the beverage equivalent of a rollercoaster ride.

Orbitz was, at its core, a soda — a carbonated drink with fruit flavors like raspberry, citrus, and grape — but the real magic lay in the suspended globules. These little, wobbly spheres were made of gellan, a gelatinous substance derived from seaweed. The gel-like balls added a chewy texture that turned a regular drink into something entirely more interactive.

The drink's liquid itself was a classic sugary soda — nothing too fancy, just a fun, fruity burst of flavor. It wasn't overly sweet, which made the texture of the orbs even more of a fun contrast. As you drank, the chewy globules would float up and down in the soda, creating an unexpectedly satisfying experience.

While some folks were skeptical about the whole "soda with floating orbs" concept, those who gave it a chance found themselves enchanted by the playful vibe. It was a drink that invited curiosity, like it had jumped straight out of a quirky science fiction movie. It wasn't just something to drink — it was something to experience.

While the floating orbs might have seemed like a strange addition at first glance, they actually complemented the soda's flavor quite well. The fruitiness of the soda paired nicely with the gelatinous texture. It was like a candy and a soda combined into one. The chewy bits were gentle and not overpowering — just enough to add a bit of fun and texture to your drink without distracting from the flavor.

There was something undeniably satisfying about sipping on a cold bottle of Orbitz. You'd shake it up, watch the orbs dance, and then take a sip, expecting the usual sugary soda experience. But instead, you'd get a burst of fruit flavor and a unique texture that made the whole act of drinking feel more engaging.

It wasn't the taste that set Orbitz apart — it was the full, immersive experience. Orbitz took something as simple as soda and transformed it into something that felt new, fresh, and exciting. It wasn't just about quenching your thirst — it was about discovering a new kind of fun.

Sadly, Orbitz soda didn't stick around for long. After a year of enthusiastic hype, it was pulled from shelves in 1998. Why? Well, it wasn't because people didn't love it — it was actually the opposite. Orbitz was too innovative, too unique, and too ahead of its time. The public, while intrigued, wasn't ready for a soda with gelatinous orbs floating around in it. Some just didn't know how to approach it. It was a little too odd for the mainstream at that moment.

There was also the challenge of consumer habits. People loved their familiar, simple sodas, and Orbitz's strange new texture wasn't something everyone could get used to. But in retrospect, it's clear that Orbitz was a drink that was ahead of its time — perhaps even too bold for the late 90s. In an era where people were just getting used to bottled water and flavored sodas, Orbitz was the drink that challenged norms and invited you to look at beverages in a new light.

Though Orbitz may have disappeared from the shelves after only a brief stint, it still holds a special place in the hearts of 90s kids. For those who remember it, Orbitz is the epitome of the "weird, but fun" spirit of the decade. It was a drink that wasn't afraid to be different, to stand out, and to offer an entirely new type of drinking experience.

Orbitz's brief existence may have been a blink in the larger history of beverages, but it's one that continues to spark conversation and curiosity among those who experienced it. In fact, you can still find bottles of Orbitz on resale websites, with some fetching surprisingly high prices as collectors' items. It's a testament to the drink's odd charm — and its lasting impact on the imagination of those who were lucky enough to try it.

Orbitz may have been discontinued, but it will always remain an unforgettable part of the 90s zeitgeist. It was a drink that defied expectations, invited playfulness, and offered a little glimpse into a future where soda was more than just a liquid in a bottle. Orbitz wasn't just a beverage — it was a statement about the era it came from: the '90s, a time of boundless creativity, experimental flavors, and a genuine desire to make everyday experiences feel a little more exciting.

So, here's to Orbitz — your colorful orbs and your quirky texture may have been ahead of their time, but your legacy will always live on in the hearts (and taste buds) of those who remember that strange, wonderful, and unforgettable soda.
