Bill & Ted's Excellent Cereal

A Most Awesome Breakfast Adventure.

Gone, not forgotten
MakerRalston
Years1991
StatusDiscontinued
ShelfCereals

Party on, readers: in 1991, Ralston looked at a movie about two rock-obsessed teenagers traveling through time in a phone booth, and made the only responsible decision available to a cereal company. They put it in a box. Bill & Ted's Excellent Cereal — "A Most Awesome Breakfast Adventure," cinnamon oat squares with marshmallow notes — remains the single most committed movie cereal ever produced, a tie-in so total that the box included collectible postcards from history itself, as if breakfast were a field trip and the field trip were excellent.

Read the original post on Substack

The story

Most licensed cereals borrow a character. This one borrowed a worldview. Bill & Ted did not just want you to eat their cereal; the box argued that breakfast could be a time-travel adventure, that history's great figures endorsed it, and that the correct response to a bowl of cinnamon oat squares was the response the boys had to everything: excellent.

It holds a place of genuine honor at www.90sfoodie.com, because no cereal has ever believed in its own premise harder.

Station! The Most Triumphant Box Design Ever

Read the box the way it begs to be read — as a poster first and a grocery item second. The sky-blue panel is a riot: Bill and Ted themselves flanking their time-traveling phone booth in mid-celebration, Rufus emerging from the booth like a roadie saint, a roller coaster curling in the distance for no structural reason, and the title in the movie's signature yellow-and-red lettering so large it crowds the word "NEW" into a corner. Beneath the boys, the cereal itself: golden cinnamon oat squares studded with green marshmallow music notes. And in the corner, the box within the box — the "Hysterical Historical Post Cards," free inside, "Collect All 4 Dudes!"

Every inch of that design is fluent in the source material. The phone booth is not set dressing; it is the vehicle of the entire franchise, the TARDIS of San Dimas. The marshmallow notes are not a random shape; Bill & Ted's whole destiny was a song that would unite the world, and here is that song, floating in milk. Even the cereal's base had a logic to it — sturdy cinnamon oat squares, the kind of breakfast two dudes living on snacks and destiny would actually eat. Compare that care to the average movie cereal of the era, in which a character's face was glued onto generic fruity rings, and the difference is the difference between a costume and a commitment. Readers write to www.90sfoodie.com about this box with the tone people use for a beloved local band: you had to be there, and if you were, you never really left.

Postcards From History, Some Assembly Required

The postcards deserve their own paragraph, because they were the most Bill & Ted promotion imaginable. The movies' great running engine was the abduction of historical figures — Napoleon, Socrates, Billy the Kid, Joan of Arc — hauled through time for a school report. The cereal turned that into a collectible set of "Hysterical Historical" postcards, one in each box, trading-card style, which a kid was meant to gather the way the boys gathered the dudes. It was educational content wearing a clown nose, and it landed in the precise sweet spot of the era's marketing: a prize that made parents feel the purchase was vaguely scholarly while kids treated it as contraband from the coolest movie alive.

This was the tie-in cereal as total environment. The movie sent teenagers through history; the cereal sent postcards from history into your kitchen. The box was not advertising the film. It was extending it — another episode, served cold with milk. We keep a file for cereals that built worlds instead of renting faces, and this one sits on the first page, in the booth, with the door open.

A Cereal From the Peak of the Movie-Tie-In Age

Context matters here, because Bill & Ted's Excellent Cereal arrived at the absolute high-water mark of the licensed cereal era, when Ralston — the unsung king of tie-in breakfasts — would build a cereal around nearly anything with a pulse and a Saturday morning slot. The boys were perfect raw material: a hit movie, a sequel in theaters, an animated series on television, and a catchphrase vocabulary that every kid in America could already speak. "Excellent," "Bogus," "Party on, dudes" — the cereal did not need to teach the customer anything. It just needed to be on the shelf while the wave was up.

The wave, of course, went out the way movie waves do. The sequel passed, the cartoon ended, the franchise settled into beloved-classic status, and a cereal tied that tightly to a moment had no second act available. Excellent Cereal was discontinued as quietly as a phone booth slipping out of a parking lot, and its afterlife began: the box became one of the great collector's items of the cereal world, the photograph that launches a thousand "they made THAT into a cereal?" conversations.

Here is the thing, though — the franchise came back. Bill & Ted returned to theaters decades later, older, kinder, and still excellent, and an entire generation discovered the movies through their parents. The cereal did not return with them, which leaves Excellent Cereal in the purest category the archive tracks at www.90sfoodie.com: a product whose source material is alive and beloved, and which exists now only in the memory of people who ate breakfast in 1991 and have been waiting, patiently, to be bodaciously fed again.

Be Excellent to Each Bowl

The final judgment is the boys' own philosophy, which the cereal embodied better than it had any obligation to: be excellent to each other — and, the box would add, to breakfast. Excellent Cereal treated a kids' movie with total respect, treated its young customers as initiates rather than targets, and treated cinnamon oat squares as a vehicle worthy of history's greatest figures. Most cereals sell sugar. This one sold a worldview with marshmallow notes in it.

So, the question we keep in the movie-cereal file at www.90sfoodie.com: does Bill & Ted's Excellent Cereal take the crown as the greatest movie tie-in cereal of all time — and if not, who dethrones the Wyld Stallyns? And if the boys' long-awaited return era had brought the cereal back for one more ride, postcards included, are you collecting all four dudes at your current age? Be honest. We know the answer.
