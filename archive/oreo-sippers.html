Oreo Sippers

The cookie straw you drank your milk through.

Gone, not forgotten
MakerNabisco
Years2000s–2012
StatusDiscontinued
ShelfCandy

There was a brief, beautiful era when the snack world looked at a drinking straw and asked a bold question: what if the straw was also dessert? Yogurt came in tubes. Cereal came in straws. And Nabisco, never one to let a trend pass the cookie aisle by, gave us the Oreo Sipper — a hollow wafer straw rolled out of the Oreo itself. You stuck it in a glass of ice-cold milk, sipped, and the milk came up through the cookie. Then you ate the straw. Genius has rarely been this simple, or this crunchy.

The story

The Sippers came in twin-wrapped packs, two straws to a wrapper, in boxes like the ones in this photo — the classic chocolate Oreo on the blue box, and the Golden Oreo Sipper on the yellow, which a lot of us quietly considered the superior instrument. The wafer was crisp and chocolatey, rolled tight enough to hold together in milk… for a while. Because technique mattered. Sip too slowly and your straw went soft, sagged, and staged a small collapse into the glass. Sip with confidence and you got that perfect middle moment — cold milk, softened cookie, one bite of pure Saturday morning — before finishing the straw itself like the world's most satisfying cleanup. This is exactly the kind of snack we keep filed in the archive at 90sfoodie.com, because a snack you could play with deserves to be remembered properly.

The Sippers rode in at the height of the great edible-utensil era, that late-90s-into-2000s stretch when snacks doubled as activities. Dunkaroos made dunking a sport. Go-Gurt made yogurt portable. Cereal straws turned breakfast milk into a prize. The Oreo Sipper fit right in: it took the most ritual snack in America — the Oreo, already famous for twist, lick, dunk — and added one more verb. Sip. Search any shelf of discontinued snacks worth its salt and you'll find the Sippers near the top of the most-missed list, right alongside the big names we track at 90sfoodie.com.

Now, the debate that still starts arguments: chocolate or Golden? Team Chocolate says the original is the only true Sipper — dark wafer, cold milk, that cookies-and-cream rush in every pull. Team Golden counters that the vanilla wafer straw was the smoother sip, sweeter, gentler, and frankly better looking in the glass. There is no wrong answer, only a revealing one. Tell us your team in the comments — polls like this are the heartbeat of 90sfoodie.com, and the Golden-versus-chocolate debate has never officially been settled.

Then, around 2012, the Sippers quietly vanished. No announcement, no farewell tour — one of those slow disappearances where you check the cookie aisle a few times, then a few times more, then realize. Fans noticed. Petitions circulated. People asked Nabisco directly, and the company's answer on social media was pure cliffhanger: never say never. In the years since, we've documented a lot of quiet goodbyes like this one at 90sfoodie.com, and the Sipper's file stays open, because snacks with this kind of fan loyalty have a habit of surprising us.

There is a near-relative still out there: Japan's Oreo Wafer Rolls look the part and taste the part, rolled wafers with that familiar cookie crunch. What they don't do is sip — they're a tribute, not the tool. The original Sipper wasn't just flavor, it was function. It was engineering you could eat. And in an era when discontinued favorites keep staging comebacks because fans refuse to let go, a milk-ready cookie straw feels less like a memory and more like a product waiting for its second act. If it ever happens, you'll read about it at 90sfoodie.com — probably within the hour, because some of us never stopped checking the cookie aisle.

So tell us: were you a sipper, or did you skip the milk and eat the straws dry like cookies with ambitions? Team Chocolate or Team Golden?
