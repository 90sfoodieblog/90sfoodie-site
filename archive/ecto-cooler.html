Hi-C Ecto Cooler

The green juice box that became the king of discontinued drinks.

Gone, not forgotten
MakerHi-C (Coca-Cola)
YearsLate 1980s–2001 · revived 2016 & 2021
StatusDiscontinued
ShelfDrinks

It was green, it was citrus, and the mascot on the box was a ghost famous for eating everything in the building. Hi-C Ecto Cooler should not have worked at all. Instead, it became the most missed juice box in America — the discontinued drink against which every other discontinued drink is measured.

The story

Most movie tie-in foods are fireworks: bright, loud, gone by the sequel. Ecto Cooler broke the pattern so completely that it rewrote what a tie-in could be. It outlived its cartoon, outlived its century, got cancelled, got resurrected by popular demand, and still — still — tops the lists of products people beg a company to bring back. Coca-Cola has discontinued thousands of flavors. This is the one the internet will not let them forget.

It holds a permanent place of honor at www.90sfoodie.com, because Ecto Cooler is not really a story about a drink. It is a story about what happens when a flavor gets adopted by a generation as personal property.

A Cartoon You Could Drink

The setup sounds like a dare. In the late 1980s, Hi-C had a citrus punch and The Real Ghostbusters — the animated spin-off of the Ghostbusters films — had a breakout star: Slimer, the green, gluttonous, entirely lovable ghost. The flavor was rechristened Ecto Cooler, after the ectoplasm Slimer trailed everywhere, dyed green, and put into juice boxes with Slimer himself grinning on the front, thumb up, an orange orbiting beside him like a planet he had not eaten yet.

Everything about it was aimed with precision at the exact kid standing in the grocery aisle. The color was impossible — drinks were not supposed to be that green, which was of course the entire appeal. The name was a joke you got only if you watched the show, which made drinking it feel like membership. And the box did the thing the best tie-ins do: it did not taste like a promotion. Kids who had never seen a Ghostbuster still fought over the green juice box in the variety pack. Slimer was not selling a movie. Slimer was selling citrus, forever.

Here is the detail that proves the flavor had real bones: the citrus punch underneath the branding had been around in Hi-C's lineup long before Slimer touched it. Ecto Cooler was a good drink wearing a great costume. That combination — substance under the spectacle — is why it survived its own licensing deal by more than a decade, still sitting in lunchboxes long after Saturday morning had moved on.

What Ecto Cooler Actually Tasted Like

Descriptions of Ecto Cooler from memory tend to glow green and say "citrus," so let us be precise. It was a tangerine-orange citrus drink — sweet up front, with the particular tang of juice-box citrus, and its signature move was psychological: it was green, and your brain insisted green should taste like lime, so every sip carried a tiny, pleasant argument between what your eyes expected and what your tongue reported. A whole generation learned flavor dissonance from a juice box and called it delicious.

The packaging played its part perfectly. The 6.75-ounce box was the standard unit of childhood refreshment — small straw, foil dot, three committed stabs to get in. Ecto Cooler came in those boxes by the multipack and in bigger cans for the home fridge, and the green inside was not the murky green of health drinks. It was cartoon green. Nuclear green. Slimer green. Readers describing it to us reach for the same words decade after decade — bright, electric, radioactive — about a fruit drink that contained ten percent juice and one hundred percent confidence.

The Disappearance, in Slow Motion

Hi-C did not kill Ecto Cooler in a day; it faded it, which somehow made things worse. Around 2001, the Ecto Cooler name disappeared from shelves — but the drink itself lingered on for a while under a new identity, Shoutin' Orange Tangergreen, with Slimer gone and the magic draining out of the box in direct proportion to the licensing. Later reformulations and renames followed, and by the late 2000s the flavor was gone entirely, in every costume.

That slow fade taught fans a lesson the internet would soon weaponize: discontinued does not have to mean forgotten. Ecto Cooler became the founding legend of drink nostalgia — the reference point in every thread, the name invoked whenever any company retired any flavor. "Remember Ecto Cooler" stopped being a memory and became an argument: proof that a beloved product could persist in the culture long after the supply chain gave up on it. The archive at www.90sfoodie.com tracks discontinued drinks the way other sites track sports dynasties, and Ecto Cooler is the dynasty.

The Revival That Proved the Rule

Then came the experiment that settled every debate about whether nostalgia could move product. In 2016, with a new Ghostbusters film arriving, Coca-Cola brought Ecto Cooler back — juice boxes and cans, Slimer and all, as a limited release. The response was not polite retro interest. It was a stampede. Supplies vanished, prices on resale listings climbed to absurd heights, and the revival became a news story in its own right. When Ghostbusters: Afterlife arrived in 2021, Ecto Cooler surfaced again in a limited promotional run, because by then the ritual was established: a new Ghostbusters means the green drink rises.

Notice what that history really shows. Ecto Cooler did not come back because a company ran the numbers on a citrus punch. It came back because the demand had been visibly, measurably building for fifteen years in petitions, threads, and Halloween costumes. Along with a small handful of other legends — your Surges, your Crystal Pepsis — it became the template for the modern nostalgia revival: the fans keep the brand warm for free, for decades, until the math finally works. The threads at www.90sfoodie.com about Ecto Cooler read less like reminiscing and more like a standing order, still open.

The Juice Box Crown

So why this drink, of all drinks? Because Ecto Cooler hit the exact trifecta that makes a product unforgettable: a flavor kids genuinely liked, a character they genuinely loved, and a color they were never supposed to see in a beverage. Remove any leg and it is a footnote. Together, they built the drink that defined what "discontinued" even means to the internet — the green ghost in every comment section, asking to come home.

Our question for the comments, the one that runs whenever the Ecto Cooler file opens at www.90sfoodie.com: is Ecto Cooler your number one discontinued drink of all time — and if not, what dethrones a green juice box with a ghost on it? Bonus argument, free of charge: juice box or can, because households divided over this in 2016 and some have not reconciled.
