Mascot Mania, Round 3

Mascot Mania, Round 3 deals in the verifiable: who made it, when it lived, what it promised on the box. Ten questions, one printed answer key, zero guesses — in Mascot Mania, Round 3, the record does the talking.

  1. Popcorn Chicken came from KFC. Which of these did too?
    A. KFC Chicken Littles
    B. TMNT Pudding Pies
    C. Rice Krispies
    D. Lemonhead
  2. What was the famous line attached to Cocoa Puffs?
    A. Cuckoo for Cocoa Puffs
    B. Made from the best stuff on Earth
    C. Be like Mike
    D. That's Brisk, baby!
  3. Which company gave us Boo Berry?
    A. Little Debbie
    B. Cry Baby
    C. Ouch!
    D. General Mills
  4. Which aisle of the 90s did Dunkaroos call home?
    A. Cereals
    B. Fast Food
    C. Snack Cakes & Cookies
    D. Lunchbox
  5. Which company gave us Tato Skins?
    A. Capri Sun
    B. Totino's
    C. Keebler
    D. Cry Baby
  6. Who fronted Urkel-O's in the ads?
    A. Fred Flintstone
    B. Finn the Goldfish
    C. Little Debbie
    D. Steve Urkel
  7. Cheetos came from Frito-Lay. Which of these did too?
    A. King Vitaman
    B. Burger King Kids Club
    C. Viennetta
    D. Cooler Ranch Doritos
  8. Which debuted first — Fruit Stripe, Pillsbury Doughboy, Ding Dongs, or Cap'n Crunch?
    A. Fruit Stripe
    B. Ding Dongs
    C. Cap'n Crunch
    D. Pillsbury Doughboy
  9. Which debuted first — Cheetos, The Noid, Andy Capp's Hot Fries, or Quisp?
    A. The Noid
    B. Quisp
    C. Cheetos
    D. Andy Capp's Hot Fries
  10. Which of these vanished from the shelves — Book It!, Crispy M&M's, Dum Dums, or Otter Pops?
    A. Crispy M&M's
    B. Otter Pops
    C. Dum Dums
    D. Book It!

The Answer Key

  1. A. KFC Chicken Littles — KFC, 1986–1990. Tiny chicken sandwiches for pocket change, gone too soon, revived decades later as Littles.
  2. A. Cocoa Puffs — General Mills, 1958. Sonny went cuckoo in every 90s commercial break.
  3. D. Boo Berry — General Mills, 1973. The blueberry ghost cereal that completed the monster trio.
  4. C. Dunkaroos — Betty Crocker, 1990–2012. Cookies and frosting dip — the lunchbox legend, revived in 2020 after an 8-year absence.
  5. C. Tato Skins — Keebler, 1985. Potato-skin chips in cheddar and bacon, from the hollow tree.
  6. D. Urkel-O's — Ralston, 1991–1992. A Family Matters cereal — did it do that? It did, briefly.
  7. D. Cooler Ranch Doritos — Frito-Lay, 1986. Launched as Cooler Ranch in 1986; renamed Cool Ranch for international shelves.
  8. A. Fruit Stripe — Fruit Stripe, 1960–2024. Tattooed stripes on every stick; the flavor was famously brief.
  9. C. Cheetos — Frito-Lay, 1948. Chester Cheetah took over the ads in 1986, too cool for the original mouse.
  10. A. Crispy M&M's — Mars, 1999–2005. The orange-shelled crispy M&M, gone from the US in 2005 and revived a decade later.
