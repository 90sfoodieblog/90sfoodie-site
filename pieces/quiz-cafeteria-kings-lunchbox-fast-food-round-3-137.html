Cafeteria Kings: Lunchbox & Fast Food, Round 3

Cafeteria Kings: Lunchbox & Fast Food, Round 3 deals in the verifiable: who made it, when it lived, what it promised on the box. Ten questions, one printed answer key, zero guesses — in Cafeteria Kings: Lunchbox & Fast Food, Round 3, the record does the talking.

The Answer Key

  1. A. Jell-O Pudding Snacks — Jell-O, 1980. Pudding pops' cup cousins in the refrigerated case.
  2. D. Lunchables — Oscar Mayer, 1988. Build-your-own turkey, cheese, and crackers — the lunchbox power symbol from 1988.
  3. D. Boston Market — Boston Market, 1985. Boston Chicken's rotisserie boom renamed the chain Boston Market in 1995.
  4. D. KFC Chicken Littles — KFC, 1986–1990. Tiny chicken sandwiches for pocket change, gone too soon, revived decades later as Littles.
  5. C. Subway — Subway, 1965. Founded as Pete's Super Submarines in 1965; the 90s made it the 'fresh' alternative.
  6. C. Crazy Bread — Little Caesars, 1982. Butter-and-parmesan breadsticks with Crazy Sauce.
  7. C. McDonald's Happy Meal — McDonald's, 1979. The boxed meal with a toy — the toy became the point by the 90s.
  8. B. Chick-fil-A Chicken Sandwich — Chick-fil-A, 1964. Pressure-cooked with pickle chips; the mall food court's southern anchor.
  9. A. McDonald's Fried Apple Pie — McDonald's, 1968–1992. The original pie was fried until 1992, when the baked version took over in the US.
  10. C. The Whopper — Burger King, 1957. The flame-grilled standard since 1957.
