The Vault, Round 13: Gone But Not Forgotten

The Vault, Round 13: Gone But Not Forgotten deals in the verifiable: who made it, when it lived, what it promised on the box. Ten questions, one printed answer key, zero guesses — in The Vault, Round 13: Gone But Not Forgotten, the record does the talking.

  1. Fruitopia Fruit Integration came from Coca-Cola. Which of these did too?
    A. Surge
    B. Pixy Stix
    C. Cry Baby
    D. Tongue Splashers
  2. When did McDonald's Fried Apple Pie leave the shelves?
    A. 1992
    B. 1988
    C. 1999
    D. 1997
  3. Which aisle of the 90s did Fruity Yummy Mummy call home?
    A. Fast Food
    B. Frozen & Ice Cream
    C. Cereals
    D. Snacks & Chips

    B. The Whopper
    C. The Noid

    A. Big Mac
    B. Boston Market
    C. KFC Chicken Littles
    D. Sonic Drive-In
  6. In what year did Necco Wafers first reach shelves?

    A. Reptar Crunch

    C. OK Soda

  10. Which of these vanished from the shelves — Honey Nut Cheerios, Nik-L-Nip, Pepsi A.M., or Popcorn Chicken?
    A. Popcorn Chicken
    B. Nik-L-Nip
    C. Pepsi A.M.
    D. Honey Nut Cheerios

The Answer Key

  1. A. Surge — Coca-Cola, 1996–2003. Coke's citrus answer to Mountain Dew; fans campaigned it back in 2014.
  2. A. McDonald's Fried Apple Pie — McDonald's, 1968–1992. The original pie was fried until 1992, when the baked version took over in the US.
  3. C. Fruity Yummy Mummy — General Mills, 1987–1992. The fifth monster cereal, gone before the decade turned.
  4. B. The Whopper — Burger King, 1957. The flame-grilled standard since 1957.
  5. C. KFC Chicken Littles — KFC, 1986–1990. Tiny chicken sandwiches for pocket change, gone too soon, revived decades later as Littles.
  6. B. Necco Wafers — Necco, 1847–2018. The chalky wafer rolls of every glove box; the 2018 shutdown scare emptied shelves.
  7. A. Clearly Canadian — Clearly Canadian, 1987. Sparkling flavored water in the tall glass bottle, a convenience-store status drink.
  8. A. Jolt Cola — Jolt, 1985–2009. The original energy cola, sold on a single boast.
  9. D. Slice — Pepsi, 1984–2009. Pepsi's juice-blended soda line; its lemon-lime handed the torch to Sierra Mist in 1999.
  10. C. Pepsi A.M. — Pepsi, 1989–1990. A higher-caffeine 'morning cola,' gone within a year.
