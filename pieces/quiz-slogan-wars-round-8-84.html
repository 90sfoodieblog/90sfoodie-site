Slogan Wars, Round 8

The Slogan Wars, Round 8 round: ten questions from the archive's files, each with its answer waiting in the key below. In Slogan Wars, Round 8 the facts were old before they were trivia — that is what makes them fair game.

  1. Name the product in this memory: “The pitcher who burst through walls; Bursts bottles joined the pouches in the 90s.”
    A. SunnyD
    B. Kool-Aid Bursts
    C. Faygo
    D. Kool-Aid
  2. Which product promised: “Yabba Dabba Doo”?
    A. Rice Krispies Treats Cereal
    B. Bubble Yum
    C. Fruity Pebbles
    D. Bigfoot Pizza
  3. Which aisle of the 90s did Sour Patch Kids call home?
    A. Candy
    B. Gum
    C. Snack Cakes & Cookies
    D. Snacks & Chips
  4. Name the product in this memory: “Tony's been roaring since 1952.”
    A. Trix
    B. Frosted Flakes
    C. Corn Pops
    D. Rice Krispies
  5. Which aisle of the 90s did Mountain Dew call home?
    A. Sodas & Drinks
    B. Frozen & Ice Cream
    C. Snacks & Chips
    D. Lunchbox
  6. Which company gave us Snapple?
    A. Smucker's
    B. Snapple
    C. Pepsi
    D. KFC
  7. Taco Bell Chihuahua came from Taco Bell. Which of these did too?
    A. Otter Pops
    B. Taco Bell Value Menu
    C. Ring Pop
    D. Uncrustables
  8. Which debuted first — Taco Bell Value Menu, Kit Kat, Mountain Dew, or Crystal Pepsi?
    A. Taco Bell Value Menu
    B. Kit Kat
    C. Crystal Pepsi
    D. Mountain Dew
  9. Which debuted first — Kool-Aid, Wheaties, Brisk Iced Tea, or Dippin' Dots?
    A. Kool-Aid
    B. Wheaties
    C. Brisk Iced Tea
    D. Dippin' Dots
  10. Which of these can you still buy today — Batman Cereal, Domino's 30-Minute Guarantee, Golden Crisp, or Hydrox?
    A. Batman Cereal
    B. Domino's 30-Minute Guarantee
    C. Golden Crisp
    D. Hydrox

The Answer Key

  1. D. Kool-Aid — Kraft, 1927. The pitcher who burst through walls; Bursts bottles joined the pouches in the 90s.
  2. C. Fruity Pebbles — Post, 1971. Fred and Barney's fruity flakes; the milk turned pink.
  3. A. Sour Patch Kids — Mondelēz, 1985. First sold as Mars Men in the 70s; the sour-then-sweet switch was the hook.
  4. B. Frosted Flakes — Kellogg's, 1952. Tony's been roaring since 1952.
  5. A. Mountain Dew — Pepsi, 1948. The extreme-sports era's fuel; the 90s made it a lifestyle.
  6. B. Snapple — Snapple, 1972. Bottled teas and juices whose radio ads made Wendy a folk hero.
  7. B. Taco Bell Value Menu — Taco Bell, 1988. The 59-79-99 era made Taco Bell the broke teenager's banquet.
  8. B. Kit Kat — Hershey, 1935. The break-apart bar with the jingle everyone can finish.
  9. B. Wheaties — General Mills, 1924. The orange box put Michael Jordan on more breakfast tables than anyone.
  10. C. Golden Crisp — Post, 1949. Sugar Bear crooned it, cool as a jazz singer.
