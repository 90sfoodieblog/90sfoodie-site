The Drink Tank, Round 2

This is The Drink Tank, Round 2 — a ten-question round built entirely from documented shelf history. The Drink Tank, Round 2 asks no trick questions and repeats no rumors: the archive only asks what it can prove. The The Drink Tank, Round 2 key is printed at the end, the archive way.

    A. Gum
    B. Snacks & Chips
    C. Cereals
    D. Sodas & Drinks
  2. In what year did Clearly Canadian first reach shelves?
    A. 1977
    B. 1980
    C. 1987
    D. 1997

  10. Which of these can you still buy today — Fruit Stripe, Necco Wafers, Reptar Crunch, or Snapple?

    B. Necco Wafers
    C. Fruit Stripe

The Answer Key

  1. D. Tampico — Tampico, 1989. Gallon citrus punch — the cookout and corner-store staple from 1989.
  2. C. Clearly Canadian — Clearly Canadian, 1987. Sparkling flavored water in the tall glass bottle, a convenience-store status drink.
  3. D. Hawaiian Punch — Hawaiian Punch, 1934. Punchy offered a punch, and took one, in every spot.
  4. B. SunnyD — Sunny Delight, 1963. The orange drink kids pulled from the fridge while parents reached for 'the purple stuff' (in the famous ad, the sunny one won).
  5. A. Kool-Aid — Kraft, 1927. The pitcher who burst through walls; Bursts bottles joined the pouches in the 90s.
  6. D. Powerade — Coca-Cola, 1988. Coke's sports drink, launched into Gatorade's decade.
  7. D. Brisk Iced Tea — Lipton, 1991. Claymation celebrity ads made Brisk a 90s catchphrase machine.
  8. C. Arizona Iced Tea — Arizona, 1992. The tall 99-cent can that never raised its price.
  9. A. Mr. Pibb — Coca-Cola, 1972–2001. The Dr Pepper rival renamed Pibb Xtra in 2001.
  10. D. Snapple — Snapple, 1972. Bottled teas and juices whose radio ads made Wendy a folk hero.
