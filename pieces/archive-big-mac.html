Big Mac

McDonald's · 1968 – present

Big Mac sits in the fast food file because the 90s are unthinkable with its slot left empty. McDonald's first sold it in 1968, and from that year forward Big Mac was simply part of how the decade stocked itself.

The card for Big Mac reads as follows: a McDonald's product of the fast food shelf, first sold in 1968, and it is still sold today. It is a short card, and deliberately so — the archive learned long ago that the facts of Big Mac survive best when they are stated once, exactly, and without decoration. The decoration around Big Mac is real, but it lives in the other paragraphs; this one is the spine they hang from.

Nobody handed Big Mac its place; it took the place the slow way, appearing in one lunchbox, then three, then every row of the cafeteria. The jingle lists the whole build; a Pittsburgh creation that went national in 1968.

At full height, Big Mac stopped competing with its shelf-mates and started being compared to its own past self — the surest sign that Big Mac had become the standard of its aisle. The line attached to Big Mac — “Two all-beef patties, special sauce, lettuce, cheese” — is still quoted by people who have not seen the product in years.

The staying power of Big Mac needs no theory beyond itself — it is still there, on the same errand it ran in the 90s. Products are discontinued every week; Big Mac simply never was.

The natural habitat of Big Mac was the ordinary afternoon — homework half-done, cartoons queued, somebody's bike still lying in the driveway. Special-occasion foods get ceremonies; Big Mac got Tuesdays. A food like Big Mac, owning Tuesdays, owns the decade's memory outright.

Big Mac left instructions behind. Every retro revival that works — every reissue that sells out in a weekend — is following the map that Big Mac drew: be specific, be generous, be gone or be forever. If the 90s ever get a museum, Big Mac will not need a plaque. Big Mac will need a shelf, restocked daily, forever slightly picked over.

The archive also notes what Big Mac was not: not a stunt, not a seasonal visitor, not a licensing afterthought. Big Mac was a working product with a maker's name — McDonald's — printed on it in full view, and working products are the ones that end up carrying a decade's ordinary happiness on their backs.

Everything the record can say about Big Mac has now been said in one place, probably for the first time. If a reader came here to settle a bet about Big Mac, the archive hopes the loser pays gracefully.
