Mascot Mania, Round 9

Mascot Mania, Round 9 deals in the verifiable: who made it, when it lived, what it promised on the box. Ten questions, one printed answer key, zero guesses — in Mascot Mania, Round 9, the record does the talking.

  1. Which company gave us Count Chocula?
    A. Jolt
    B. Frigo
    C. General Mills
    D. Dove
  2. In what year did Little Debbie Swiss Rolls first reach shelves?
    A. 1961
    B. 1968
    C. 1963
    D. 1988
  3. Name the product in this memory: “Dig 'Em hopped onto the box in 1972; the cereal was Sugar Smacks before 1984.”
    A. Wheaties
    B. Fruity Pebbles
    C. Morning Funnies
    D. Honey Smacks
  4. Which company gave us Rice Krispies?
    A. Kellogg's
    B. Jell-O
    C. Jack in the Box
    D. American Licorice
  5. Which company gave us Scooby-Doo Fruit Snacks?
    A. Otter Pops
    B. Betty Crocker
    C. Just Born
    D. Pizza Hut
  6. In what year did Nutty Bars first reach shelves?
    A. 1967
    B. 1961
    C. 1964
    D. 1959
  7. Which product promised: “Follow my nose”?
    A. Chips Ahoy!
    B. Froot Loops
    C. Sno Balls
    D. Cherry Coke
  8. Which of these can you still buy today — Bigfoot Pizza, Fruitopia Fruit Integration, Good & Plenty, or Wendy's SuperBar?
    A. Fruitopia Fruit Integration
    B. Wendy's SuperBar
    C. Good & Plenty
    D. Bigfoot Pizza
  9. Which of these can you still buy today — Domino's 30-Minute Guarantee, Jell-O Pudding Snacks, Keebler Magic Middles, or The Noid?
    A. Domino's 30-Minute Guarantee
    B. Jell-O Pudding Snacks
    C. Keebler Magic Middles
    D. The Noid
  10. Which of these can you still buy today — Jell-O Pudding Snacks, Josta, Sprinkle Spangles, or Wendy's SuperBar?
    A. Jell-O Pudding Snacks
    B. Josta
    C. Wendy's SuperBar
    D. Sprinkle Spangles

The Answer Key

  1. C. Count Chocula — General Mills, 1971. The chocolate monster cereal, a Halloween-season staple.
  2. C. Little Debbie Swiss Rolls — Little Debbie, 1963. The budget pinwheel that filled more lunchboxes than any rival.
  3. D. Honey Smacks — Kellogg's, 1953. Dig 'Em hopped onto the box in 1972; the cereal was Sugar Smacks before 1984.
  4. A. Rice Krispies — Kellogg's, 1928. The elves' names are the sound of the cereal in milk.
  5. B. Scooby-Doo Fruit Snacks — Betty Crocker, 1999. The opaque blue Scooby gummy was a flavor mystery of its own.
  6. C. Nutty Bars — Little Debbie, 1964. Peanut butter wafers in chocolate, sold as a two-stick team.
  7. B. Froot Loops — Kellogg's, 1963. Toucan Sam followed his nose; the loops were all one flavor despite the colors.
  8. C. Good & Plenty — Hershey, 1893. Licorice pellets in pink and white, shaken like a train by Choo Choo Charlie.
  9. B. Jell-O Pudding Snacks — Jell-O, 1980. Pudding pops' cup cousins in the refrigerated case.
  10. A. Jell-O Pudding Snacks — Jell-O, 1980. Pudding pops' cup cousins in the refrigerated case.
