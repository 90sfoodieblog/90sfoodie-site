The Breakfast Club, Round 1

The The Breakfast Club, Round 1 round: ten questions from the archive's files, each with its answer waiting in the key below. In The Breakfast Club, Round 1 the facts were old before they were trivia — that is what makes them fair game.

  1. In what year did Lucky Charms first reach shelves?
    A. 1939
    B. 1964
    C. 1966
    D. 1960
  2. Name the product in this memory: “The cosmic rice-crisp and caramel disc that pre-dated Cosmic Brownies.”
    A. Fig Newtons
    B. Mrs. Fields Cookies
    C. Little Debbie Star Crunch
    D. Drake's Devil Dogs
  3. Which company gave us Twinkies?
    A. Hostess
    B. Bug Juice
    C. Ouch!
    D. Kid Cuisine
  4. Which company gave us Drake's Devil Dogs?
    A. Chipwich
    B. Drake's
    C. Hawaiian Punch
    D. Campbell's
  5. When did Hydrox leave the shelves?
    A. 2000
    B. 2005
    C. 2016
    D. 2015
  6. Which aisle of the 90s did Nilla Wafers call home?
    A. Gum
    B. Frozen & Ice Cream
    C. Snack Cakes & Cookies
    D. Candy
  7. Little Debbie Swiss Rolls came from Little Debbie. Which of these did too?
    A. The Noid
    B. Creamsicle
    C. Nutty Bars
    D. Fudgsicle
  8. Which of these can you still buy today — Franken Berry, Fruitopia Fruit Integration, TMNT Pudding Pies, or Wendy's SuperBar?
    A. TMNT Pudding Pies
    B. Wendy's SuperBar
    C. Fruitopia Fruit Integration
    D. Franken Berry
  9. Which debuted first — Peanut Butter Crunch, Cocoa Puffs, Lucky Charms, or Mallomars?
    A. Lucky Charms
    B. Cocoa Puffs
    C. Mallomars
    D. Peanut Butter Crunch
  10. Which of these vanished from the shelves — Bug Juice, Combos, Quisp, or Wendy's Frosty?
    A. Bug Juice
    B. Wendy's Frosty
    C. Combos
    D. Quisp

The Answer Key

  1. B. Lucky Charms — General Mills, 1964. The marshmallow count kept growing through the 90s — blue diamonds arrived in 1998.
  2. C. Little Debbie Star Crunch — Little Debbie, early years. The cosmic rice-crisp and caramel disc that pre-dated Cosmic Brownies.
  3. A. Twinkies — Hostess, 1930. The golden snack cake that survived even Hostess's 2012 shutdown, returning in 2013.
  4. B. Drake's Devil Dogs — Drake's, 1926. Two devil's food cakes with cream — the Northeast lunchbox dog.
  5. D. Hydrox — Hydrox, 1908–2015. The original chocolate sandwich cookie — Oreo was the copycat, in 1912.
  6. C. Nilla Wafers — Nabisco, 1898. The vanilla wafer that banana pudding cannot live without.
  7. C. Nutty Bars — Little Debbie, 1964. Peanut butter wafers in chocolate, sold as a two-stick team.
  8. D. Franken Berry — General Mills, 1971. The strawberry monster cereal, twin debut with Count Chocula.
  9. C. Mallomars — Nabisco, 1913. Chocolate marshmallow cookies sold only in cool months, so the chocolate wouldn't melt.
  10. D. Quisp — Quaker, 1965–1975. The propeller-headed alien's cereal, later an internet revival darling.
