The Answer Key

  1. A. Skor — Hershey, 1981. Thin butter toffee under milk chocolate, with a Swedish crown on the wrapper.
  2. C. Hershey's Cookies 'n' Creme — Hershey, 1994. White creme and cookie bits — the bar that broke the brown-chocolate mold.
  3. A. Fun Dip — Nestlé, 1942. A Lik-A-Stix dipped in flavored sugar — a candy delivery system.
  4. C. Sour Patch Kids — Mondelēz, 1985. First sold as Mars Men in the 70s; the sour-then-sweet switch was the hook.
  5. A. Junior Mints — Tootsie, 1949. The movie-theater mint, Seinfeld's operating-room candy in 1993.
  6. D. Mike and Ike — Just Born, 1940. The fruit-chew duo in the long box.
  7. D. Charms Blow Pop — Charms, 1973. A lollipop with gum at the center — two candies, one stick.
  8. B. Necco Wafers — Necco, 1847–2018. The chalky wafer rolls of every glove box; the 2018 shutdown scare emptied shelves.
  9. D. Pixy Stix — Nestlé, 1952. Powdered sugar in a paper straw — pure sugar, poured.
  10. A. Kit Kat — Hershey, 1935. The break-apart bar with the jingle everyone can finish.
