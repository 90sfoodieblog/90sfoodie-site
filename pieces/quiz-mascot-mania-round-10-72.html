Mascot Mania, Round 10

The Mascot Mania, Round 10 round: ten questions from the archive's files, each with its answer waiting in the key below. In Mascot Mania, Round 10 the facts were old before they were trivia — that is what makes them fair game.

  1. Name the product in this memory: “The budget pinwheel that filled more lunchboxes than any rival.”
    A. Famous Amos Cookies
    B. Animal Crackers (Barnum's)
    C. Little Debbie Swiss Rolls
    D. Fig Newtons
  2. Which product promised: “They're magically delicious”?
    A. Faygo
    B. Lucky Charms
    C. Oreo O's
    D. Quisp
  3. Name the product in this memory: “Shortbread rings striped with fudge by elves in a hollow tree.”
    A. Ho Hos
    B. Keebler Fudge Stripes
    C. Chips Ahoy!
    D. Nutty Bars
  4. In what year did Mary Jane first reach shelves?
    A. 1914
    B. 1904
    C. 1894
    D. 1934
  5. Who fronted Popcorn Chicken in the ads?
    A. Bazooka Joe
    B. Batman
    C. Colonel Sanders
    D. The Pillsbury Doughboy
  6. Which aisle of the 90s did Trix call home?
    A. Frozen & Ice Cream
    B. Cereals
    C. Gum
    D. Candy
  7. Which aisle of the 90s did Cocoa Pebbles call home?
    A. Cereals
    B. Frozen & Ice Cream
    C. Snacks & Chips
    D. Lunchbox
  8. Which of these vanished from the shelves — Pillsbury Doughboy, TCBY, The Addams Family Cereal, or Totino's Pizza Rolls?
    A. The Addams Family Cereal
    B. TCBY
    C. Pillsbury Doughboy
    D. Totino's Pizza Rolls
  9. Which debuted first — Popcorn Chicken, Keebler Magic Middles, Toaster Strudel, or Rice Krispies?
    A. Toaster Strudel
    B. Keebler Magic Middles
    C. Popcorn Chicken
    D. Rice Krispies
  10. Which of these vanished from the shelves — Corn Nuts, Dubble Bubble, Spree, or The Addams Family Cereal?
    A. The Addams Family Cereal
    B. Dubble Bubble
    C. Corn Nuts
    D. Spree

The Answer Key

  1. C. Little Debbie Swiss Rolls — Little Debbie, 1963. The budget pinwheel that filled more lunchboxes than any rival.
  2. B. Lucky Charms — General Mills, 1964. The marshmallow count kept growing through the 90s — blue diamonds arrived in 1998.
  3. B. Keebler Fudge Stripes — Keebler, early years. Shortbread rings striped with fudge by elves in a hollow tree.
  4. A. Mary Jane — Mary Jane, 1914. Peanut butter and molasses chews in the yellow wrapper.
  5. C. Popcorn Chicken — KFC, 1992. Bite-size fried chicken in a bucket-shaped cup, a test item that never left.
  6. B. Trix — General Mills, 1954. Turned fruit-shaped in 1991, the decade's defining cereal-box silhouette.
  7. A. Cocoa Pebbles — Post, 1971. The chocolate twin of the Pebbles line.
  8. A. The Addams Family Cereal — Ralston, 1991–1992. Skull-and-crossbones-shaped pieces for the 1991 movie.
  9. D. Rice Krispies — Kellogg's, 1928. The elves' names are the sound of the cereal in milk.
  10. A. The Addams Family Cereal — Ralston, 1991–1992. Skull-and-crossbones-shaped pieces for the 1991 movie.
