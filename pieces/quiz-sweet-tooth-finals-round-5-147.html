Sweet Tooth Finals, Round 5

This is Sweet Tooth Finals, Round 5 — a ten-question round built entirely from documented shelf history. Sweet Tooth Finals, Round 5 asks no trick questions and repeats no rumors: the archive only asks what it can prove. The Sweet Tooth Finals, Round 5 key is printed at the end, the archive way.

The Answer Key

  1. C. AirHeads — Perfetti Van Melle, 1985. The taffy bar kids molded into shapes before eating.
  2. C. Starburst — Mars, 1967. Fruit chews that reached America in 1967 as Starburst after a UK life as Opal Fruits.
  3. C. Crispy M&M's — Mars, 1999–2005. The orange-shelled crispy M&M, gone from the US in 2005 and revived a decade later.
  4. C. Snowcaps — Nestlé, 1948. Dark chocolate drops with white sprinkles — pure movie candy.
  5. B. Milk Duds — Hershey, 1926. Caramel 'duds' — named for the round shape the machine couldn't make.
  6. C. Necco Wafers — Necco, 1847–2018. The chalky wafer rolls of every glove box; the 2018 shutdown scare emptied shelves.
  7. B. Tongue Splashers — Tongue Splashers, 1990. The gum that painted your tongue blue or green on purpose.
  8. B. Snickers — Mars, 1930. Named for the Mars family's horse; the 90s bar of choice at every checkout.
  9. A. Dubble Bubble — Tootsie, 1928. The first bubble gum, pink since the inventor's only dye was pink.
  10. A. Rain-Blo — Rain-Blo, 1940. Rainbow gumballs from the theater vending machine row.
