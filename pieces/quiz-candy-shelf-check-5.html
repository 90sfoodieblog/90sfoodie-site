Candy: Shelf Check

Candy: Shelf Check deals in the verifiable: who made it, when it lived, what it promised on the box. Ten questions, one printed answer key, zero guesses — in Candy: Shelf Check, the record does the talking.

  1. In what year did Tootsie Roll first reach shelves?
    A. 1901
    B. 1916
    C. 1906
    D. 1896
  2. Name the product in this memory: “The sour-intensity standard, imported from Taiwan and a schoolyard dare in candy form.”
    A. Warheads
    B. Dots
    C. Peanut Butter M&M's
    D. Sixlets
  3. Which aisle of the 90s did SweeTarts call home?
    A. Cereals
    B. Fast Food
    C. Sodas & Drinks
    D. Candy
  4. Which company gave us Milk Duds?
    A. Hershey
    B. Topps
    C. Necco
    D. General Foods
  5. In what year did Red Vines first reach shelves?
    A. 1948
    B. 1952
    C. 1950
    D. 1959
  6. Which company gave us Snowcaps?
    A. Nestlé
    B. Planters
    C. Superman Ice Cream
    D. Good Humor
  7. Which aisle of the 90s did Hot Tamales call home?
    A. Candy
    B. Lunchbox
    C. Sodas & Drinks
    D. Snacks & Chips
  8. Which debuted first — Junior Mints, Bit-O-Honey, Everlasting Gobstopper, or Lemonhead?
    A. Bit-O-Honey
    B. Everlasting Gobstopper
    C. Junior Mints
    D. Lemonhead
  9. Which debuted first — Tootsie Roll, Hot Tamales, Crispy M&M's, or Dots?
    A. Dots
    B. Crispy M&M's
    C. Hot Tamales
    D. Tootsie Roll
  10. Which of these can you still buy today — Dum Dums, McDonald's Fried Apple Pie, Orbitz, or Pepsi A.M.?
    A. Orbitz
    B. McDonald's Fried Apple Pie
    C. Pepsi A.M.
    D. Dum Dums

The Answer Key

  1. D. Tootsie Roll — Tootsie, 1896. The owl's question outlived the century.
  2. A. Warheads — Impact Confections, 1993. The sour-intensity standard, imported from Taiwan and a schoolyard dare in candy form.
  3. D. SweeTarts — Nestlé, 1963. Tangy tablets from the Pixy Stix formula, pressed into coins.
  4. A. Milk Duds — Hershey, 1926. Caramel 'duds' — named for the round shape the machine couldn't make.
  5. B. Red Vines — American Licorice, 1952. West Coast red licorice, rival to the twist from the East.
  6. A. Snowcaps — Nestlé, 1948. Dark chocolate drops with white sprinkles — pure movie candy.
  7. A. Hot Tamales — Just Born, 1950. Cinnamon heat in a red capsule — Mike and Ike's spicy shadow.
  8. A. Bit-O-Honey — Bit-O-Honey, 1924. Honey taffy with almond bits — the slow chew.
  9. D. Tootsie Roll — Tootsie, 1896. The owl's question outlived the century.
  10. D. Dum Dums — Spangler, 1924. The mystery flavor is one flavor's run crossing into the next.
