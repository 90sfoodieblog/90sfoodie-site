Sweet Tooth Finals, Round 7

Sweet Tooth Finals, Round 7 deals in the verifiable: who made it, when it lived, what it promised on the box. Ten questions, one printed answer key, zero guesses — in Sweet Tooth Finals, Round 7, the record does the talking.

  1. Which company gave us Haribo Goldbears?
    A. Haribo
    B. Necco
    C. Dippin' Dots
    D. Totino's
  2. Which product promised: “First they're sour, then they're sweet”?
    A. Chuck E. Cheese
    B. Cap'n Crunch's Crunch Berries
    C. Sour Patch Kids
    D. Surge
  3. Name the product in this memory: “The gum that painted your tongue blue or green on purpose.”
    A. Bubble Jug
    B. Bubble Yum
    C. Bazooka
    D. Tongue Splashers
  4. In what year did Skor first reach shelves?
    A. 1961
    B. 1979
    C. 1977
    D. 1981
  5. Which company gave us SweeTarts?
    A. Necco
    B. Circus Peanuts
    C. Nestlé
    D. Frito-Lay
  6. Which company gave us Sixlets?
    A. Sixlets
    B. Topps
    C. Jel Sert
    D. Frigo
  7. Which aisle of the 90s did Bubble Yum call home?
    A. Fast Food
    B. Gum
    C. Frozen & Ice Cream
    D. Lunchbox
  8. Which of these vanished from the shelves — Laffy Taffy, Mallomars, Necco Wafers, or Wheaties?
    A. Necco Wafers
    B. Wheaties
    C. Mallomars
    D. Laffy Taffy
  9. Which of these can you still buy today — Burger Bundles, Fruitopia Fruit Integration, Kit Kat, or Super Size?
    A. Kit Kat
    B. Fruitopia Fruit Integration
    C. Super Size
    D. Burger Bundles
  10. Which debuted first — Nerds, Reese's Pieces, Baby Bottle Pop, or Hot Tamales?
    A. Hot Tamales
    B. Baby Bottle Pop
    C. Reese's Pieces
    D. Nerds

The Answer Key

  1. A. Haribo Goldbears — Haribo, 1922. The original gummi bears, from Bonn in 1922, in US stores by 1982.
  2. C. Sour Patch Kids — Mondelēz, 1985. First sold as Mars Men in the 70s; the sour-then-sweet switch was the hook.
  3. D. Tongue Splashers — Tongue Splashers, 1990. The gum that painted your tongue blue or green on purpose.
  4. D. Skor — Hershey, 1981. Thin butter toffee under milk chocolate, with a Swedish crown on the wrapper.
  5. C. SweeTarts — Nestlé, 1963. Tangy tablets from the Pixy Stix formula, pressed into coins.
  6. A. Sixlets — Sixlets, 1960. Candy-coated chocolate in a tube of eight-ball rows.
  7. B. Bubble Yum — Bubble Yum, 1975. The soft bubble gum that fought off a spider-egg urban legend.
  8. A. Necco Wafers — Necco, 1847–2018. The chalky wafer rolls of every glove box; the 2018 shutdown scare emptied shelves.
  9. A. Kit Kat — Hershey, 1935. The break-apart bar with the jingle everyone can finish.
  10. A. Hot Tamales — Just Born, 1950. Cinnamon heat in a red capsule — Mike and Ike's spicy shadow.
