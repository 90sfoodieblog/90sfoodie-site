Mascot Mania, Round 7

This is Mascot Mania, Round 7 — a ten-question round built entirely from documented shelf history. Mascot Mania, Round 7 asks no trick questions and repeats no rumors: the archive only asks what it can prove. The Mascot Mania, Round 7 key is printed at the end, the archive way.

  1. Who fronted Famous Amos Cookies in the ads?
    A. Dig 'Em the Frog
    B. Sydney the Kangaroo
    C. Wally Amos
    D. The Trix Rabbit
  2. Name the product in this memory: “The elves' names are the sound of the cereal in milk.”
    A. Rice Krispies
    B. Apple Jacks
    C. Wheaties
    D. Count Chocula
  3. Which aisle of the 90s did Little Debbie Star Crunch call home?
    A. Snack Cakes & Cookies
    B. Fast Food
    C. Candy
    D. Frozen & Ice Cream
  4. What was the famous line attached to Trix?
    A. Give me a break
    B. The taste you can see
    C. Silly rabbit, Trix are for kids
    D. Everything is going to be OK
  5. Which aisle of the 90s did Boo Berry call home?
    A. Snacks & Chips
    B. Cereals
    C. Candy
    D. Lunchbox
  6. Which company gave us Cocoa Pebbles?
    A. Quaker
    B. Kid Cuisine
    C. Barq's
    D. Post
  7. Name the product in this memory: “The opaque blue Scooby gummy was a flavor mystery of its own.”
    A. Lunchables
    B. Scooby-Doo Fruit Snacks
    C. Chef Boyardee Beef Ravioli
    D. Frigo Cheese Heads
  8. Which debuted first — Jell-O Pudding Snacks, Burger King Kids Club, Count Chocula, or Otter Pops?
    A. Burger King Kids Club
    B. Count Chocula
    C. Jell-O Pudding Snacks
    D. Otter Pops

    A. Keebler Magic Middles
    B. Keebler Fudge Stripes
    C. Jack in the Box Antenna Balls

  10. Which of these vanished from the shelves — Crispy M&M's, Little Caesars Pizza!Pizza!, Mrs. Fields Cookies, or Warheads?
    A. Mrs. Fields Cookies
    B. Little Caesars Pizza!Pizza!
    C. Crispy M&M's
    D. Warheads

The Answer Key

  1. C. Famous Amos Cookies — Famous Amos, 1975. Bite-size chocolate chips from a talent agent turned baker.
  2. A. Rice Krispies — Kellogg's, 1928. The elves' names are the sound of the cereal in milk.
  3. A. Little Debbie Star Crunch — Little Debbie, early years. The cosmic rice-crisp and caramel disc that pre-dated Cosmic Brownies.
  4. C. Trix — General Mills, 1954. Turned fruit-shaped in 1991, the decade's defining cereal-box silhouette.
  5. B. Boo Berry — General Mills, 1973. The blueberry ghost cereal that completed the monster trio.
  6. D. Cocoa Pebbles — Post, 1971. The chocolate twin of the Pebbles line.
  7. B. Scooby-Doo Fruit Snacks — Betty Crocker, 1999. The opaque blue Scooby gummy was a flavor mystery of its own.
  8. D. Otter Pops — Otter Pops, 1970. Freeze-it-yourself juice bars with pun-named characters.
  9. A. Keebler Magic Middles — Keebler, 1988–1995. Shortbread cookies with fudge middles — filed here by vending-machine logic.
  10. C. Crispy M&M's — Mars, 1999–2005. The orange-shelled crispy M&M, gone from the US in 2005 and revived a decade later.
