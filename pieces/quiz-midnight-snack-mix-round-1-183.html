This is Midnight Snack Mix, Round 1 — a ten-question round built entirely from documented shelf history. Midnight Snack Mix, Round 1 asks no trick questions and repeats no rumors: the archive only asks what it can prove. The Midnight Snack Mix, Round 1 key is printed at the end, the archive way.

The Answer Key

  1. C. Buncha Crunch — Nestlé, 1994. Bunched Crunch pieces for the movies, launched in 1994.
  2. C. Snapple — Snapple, 1972. Bottled teas and juices whose radio ads made Wendy a folk hero.
  3. C. Atomic Fireballs — Ferrara, 1954. The cinnamon jawbreaker that separated the brave from the wise.
  4. D. Everlasting Gobstopper — Nestlé, 1976. Willy Wonka's jawbreaker that changed colors and flavors as it shrank.
  5. A. Starburst — Mars, 1967. Fruit chews that reached America in 1967 as Starburst after a UK life as Opal Fruits.
  6. C. Junior Mints — Tootsie, 1949. The movie-theater mint, Seinfeld's operating-room candy in 1993.
  7. B. Bagel Bites — Bagel Bites, 1985. When pizza's on a bagel, you can eat pizza any time — the jingle is the record.
  8. D. Fruit Stripe — Fruit Stripe, 1960–2024. Tattooed stripes on every stick; the flavor was famously brief.
  9. A. Bazooka — Topps, 1947. The gum wrapped in a tiny comic about Bazooka Joe and his eyepatch.
  10. C. McPizza — McDonald's, 1989–2000. McDonald's pizza slowed the drive-thru to a crawl; gone by 2000 in most stores.
