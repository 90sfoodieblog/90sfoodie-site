Cereals: Aisle Legends

Cereals: Aisle Legends: ten questions from the record the archive keeps. Makers, years, mascots, slogans — if Cereals: Aisle Legends asks it, the file answered it first. In Cereals: Aisle Legends, answers wait at the bottom and the honor system is in force.

  1. In what year did Golden Grahams first reach shelves?
    A. 1991
    B. 1961
    C. 1976
    D. 1951
  2. What was the famous line attached to Cinnamon Toast Crunch?
    A. The taste you can see
    B. Nobody better lay a finger on my Butterfinger

The Answer Key

  1. C. Golden Grahams — General Mills, 1976. Honey graham squares that anchored a decade of s'mores-flavored everything.
  2. A. Cinnamon Toast Crunch — General Mills, 1984. Cinnamon-sugar squares; Crazy Squares in the modern ads.
  3. D. Bill & Ted's Excellent Cereal — Ralston, 1991–1991. A most excellent tie-in, gone as fast as the phone booth.
  4. C. Reptar Crunch — Post, 1999–1999. A limited Rugrats cereal tied to the first Rugrats movie.
  5. C. Fruity Pebbles — Post, 1971. Fred and Barney's fruity flakes; the milk turned pink.
  6. B. Golden Crisp — Post, 1949. Sugar Bear crooned it, cool as a jazz singer.
  7. B. Kix — General Mills, 1937. The first puffed corn cereal, puffed in a gun-like machine.
  8. B. Corn Pops — Kellogg's, 1950. Called Sugar Corn Pops until 1984's name-shortening era.
  9. D. Nintendo Cereal System — Ralston, 1988–1989. Two cereals in one box — Super Mario fruit and Zelda berry, split down the middle.
  10. C. Wheaties — General Mills, 1924. The orange box put Michael Jordan on more breakfast tables than anyone.
