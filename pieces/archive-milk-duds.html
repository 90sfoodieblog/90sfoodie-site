Milk Duds

Hershey · 1926 – present

The short version: Milk Duds, from Hershey, first sold in 1926, shelved with the candy. The long version is the rest of this entry — and Milk Duds earns the long version, which is more than most of its shelf-mates can say.

Before the stories, the statistics of Milk Duds: produced by Hershey, filed under candy, first sold in 1926, and it is still sold today. The archive front-loads the facts of Milk Duds for a practical reason — memory edits, but a card does not. When two fans disagree about Milk Duds at some future quiz night, this paragraph is the referee both of them agreed to in advance.

First Milk Duds was a discovery, then it was a preference, then it was a line item in the family grocery run that nobody questioned anymore. Caramel 'duds' — named for the round shape the machine couldn't make.

Ask the decade to picture the candy shelf and it pictures Milk Duds first — that was the peak, and it lasted long enough that a whole cohort of kids assumed Milk Duds was a permanent feature of the world.

Still sold today, Milk Duds has outlived the decade that made it famous without surrendering what made it famous. The packaging around Milk Duds changed; the promise didn't.

In the movie of an ordinary 90s childhood, Milk Duds is not in the highlight reel; it is in the background of every third scene, on counters and in cupboards, uncredited and indispensable. Background is not a small place — for Milk Duds, it was the whole kingdom.

The afterlife of Milk Duds is an argument that never quite ends, carried on in group chats and comment threads by people who agree on little else. That argument is the monument of Milk Duds, and it is load-bearing. And whenever the reissue rumors start — and for Milk Duds, they always start — this entry is the one they get checked against.

A field note on Milk Duds, for readers keeping score at home: it sat on the candy shelf, it answered to Hershey, and it kept the hours of an ordinary afternoon. The archive adds this note because Milk Duds rewards a second look — most legends do, once the first look has done its work and the details start asking to be counted one by one.

Consider Milk Duds officially kept: its maker named, its years fixed, its legend stated and sourced in the only ledger that bothered. Future archaeologists of the snack aisle will find Milk Duds here, spelled correctly.
