Cereals: The Deep Shelf

The Cereals: The Deep Shelf round: ten questions from the archive's files, each with its answer waiting in the key below. In Cereals: The Deep Shelf the facts were old before they were trivia — that is what makes them fair game.

  1. What was the famous line attached to Frosted Flakes?
    A. Milk's favorite cookie
    B. They're gr-r-reat!
    C. They're magically delicious
    D. Nothing says lovin' like something from the oven

    A. 1988
    B. 1985
    C. 2008
    D. 1998

    B. Wheaties
    C. Frute Brute
    D. Cap'n Crunch's Crunch Berries

The Answer Key

  1. B. Frosted Flakes — Kellogg's, 1952. Tony's been roaring since 1952.
  2. A. Nintendo Cereal System — Ralston, 1988–1989. Two cereals in one box — Super Mario fruit and Zelda berry, split down the middle.
  3. C. Fruity Yummy Mummy — General Mills, 1987–1992. The fifth monster cereal, gone before the decade turned.
  4. D. Corn Pops — Kellogg's, 1950. Called Sugar Corn Pops until 1984's name-shortening era.
  5. C. Quisp — Quaker, 1965–1975. The propeller-headed alien's cereal, later an internet revival darling.
  6. D. Kix — General Mills, 1937. The first puffed corn cereal, puffed in a gun-like machine.
  7. D. Cinnamon Toast Crunch — General Mills, 1984. Cinnamon-sugar squares; Crazy Squares in the modern ads.
  8. D. Cookie Crisp — Ralston, 1977. The cereal that promised cookies for breakfast, sold by a wizard, then a burglar.
  9. A. Morning Funnies — Ralston, 1988–1989. A cereal box printed with Sunday comics panels.
  10. B. Wheaties — General Mills, 1924. The orange box put Michael Jordan on more breakfast tables than anyone.
