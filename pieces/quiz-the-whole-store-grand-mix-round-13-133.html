The Answer Key

  1. C. Tootsie Roll — Tootsie, 1896. The owl's question outlived the century.
  2. B. Baby Bottle Pop — Topps, 1998. Dip the bottle top in the powder — the late-90s novelty anthem.
  3. D. Snapple — Snapple, 1972. Bottled teas and juices whose radio ads made Wendy a folk hero.
  4. B. Bit-O-Honey — Bit-O-Honey, 1924. Honey taffy with almond bits — the slow chew.
  5. C. Fruit by the Foot — Betty Crocker, 1991. Three feet of fruit snack, unrolled like a measuring tape.
  6. A. Bug Juice — Bug Juice, 1990. The summer-camp canteen staple in the bug-logo bottle.
  7. D. Franken Berry — General Mills, 1971. The strawberry monster cereal, twin debut with Count Chocula.
  8. D. Hubba Bubba — Wrigley, 1979. Bigger bubbles, better burps in the playground rankings.
  9. D. Burger Bundles — Burger King, 1987–1990. Mini burgers sold in six-packs — the slider era before sliders were cool.
  10. A. McDLT — McDonald's, 1984–1990. Two-chamber styrofoam packaging — Jason Alexander sang its praises in 1985.
