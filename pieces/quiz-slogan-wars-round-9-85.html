Slogan Wars, Round 9

Ten questions on Slogan Wars, Round 9 — every one drawn from the archive's own record, every answer checked against the shelf it came from. The Slogan Wars, Round 9 key waits at the bottom; no peeking, that's the rule of the rewind.

    A. 1977
    B. 1970
    C. 1952
    D. 1972
  2. What was the famous line attached to Lay's?
    A. Avoid the Noid
    B. For you, not them
    C. Betcha can't eat just one
    D. It ain't easy being cheesy

    D. Uh-oh, SpaghettiOs

    B. Frosted Flakes

    D. Jolt Cola
  10. Which of these vanished from the shelves — Combos, Creamsicle, Lemonhead, or The Noid?
    A. The Noid
    B. Lemonhead
    C. Combos
    D. Creamsicle

The Answer Key

  1. D. Snapple — Snapple, 1972. Bottled teas and juices whose radio ads made Wendy a folk hero.
  2. C. Lay's — Frito-Lay, 1938. The bet was issued in 1963 and has never been lost.
  3. A. Oreo — Nabisco, 1912. The twist-lick-dunk ritual; Double Stuf had been stacking since 1974.
  4. D. Kix — General Mills, 1937. The first puffed corn cereal, puffed in a gun-like machine.
  5. B. SunnyD — Sunny Delight, 1963. The orange drink kids pulled from the fridge while parents reached for 'the purple stuff' (in the famous ad, the sunny one won).
  6. C. Golden Crisp — Post, 1949. Sugar Bear crooned it, cool as a jazz singer.
  7. B. Twizzlers — Hershey, 1929. The twisted strawberry standard since 1929.
  8. D. Snapple — Snapple, 1972. Bottled teas and juices whose radio ads made Wendy a folk hero.
  9. B. Frosted Flakes — Kellogg's, 1952. Tony's been roaring since 1952.
  10. A. The Noid — Domino's, 1986–1995. The claymation villain who ruined pizzas — Domino's mascot of chaos.
