The Answer Key

  1. D. Little Caesars Pizza!Pizza! — Little Caesars, 1979. Two pizzas for the price of one, in the long double box on paper.
  2. B. Pringles — Kellogg's, 1968. Chips engineered as identical saddles, stacked in a can to never break. The slogan debuted in 1994.
  3. A. Honey Nut Cheerios — General Mills, 1979. The honey-nut bowl that outsold the original.
  4. A. Honey Smacks — Kellogg's, 1953. Dig 'Em hopped onto the box in 1972; the cereal was Sugar Smacks before 1984.
  5. A. Nintendo Cereal System — Ralston, 1988–1989. Two cereals in one box — Super Mario fruit and Zelda berry, split down the middle.
  6. D. Fruity Pebbles — Post, 1971. Fred and Barney's fruity flakes; the milk turned pink.
  7. C. Toaster Strudel — Pillsbury, 1985. Icing packets you applied yourself — the toaster pastry arms race of 1985.
  8. D. Rice Krispies — Kellogg's, 1928. The elves' names are the sound of the cereal in milk.
  9. B. Fun Dip — Nestlé, 1942. A Lik-A-Stix dipped in flavored sugar — a candy delivery system.
  10. D. Cap'n Crunch — Quaker, 1963. The Cap'n stayed crunchy, even in milk — the box's eternal promise.
