House of Frito-Lay

House of Frito-Lay deals in the verifiable: who made it, when it lived, what it promised on the box. Ten questions, one printed answer key, zero guesses — in House of Frito-Lay, the record does the talking.

The Answer Key

  1. D. Cooler Ranch Doritos — Frito-Lay, 1986. Launched as Cooler Ranch in 1986; renamed Cool Ranch for international shelves.
  2. A. Doritos 3D's — Frito-Lay, 1998–2004. Puffed 3D Doritos in a plastic canister — the decade's chip engineering marvel.
  3. B. Doritos — Frito-Lay, 1966. Born at Disneyland's Casa de Fritos; Nacho Cheese arrived in 1972.
  4. D. Cracker Jack — Frito-Lay, 1896. Caramel corn and peanuts with a prize in every box since 1912.
  5. B. Fritos — Frito-Lay, 1932. The corn chip that built Frito-Lay — and the walking taco's base.
  6. C. Funyuns — Frito-Lay, 1969. Onion-flavored rings that taste nothing like an onion and everything like a Funyun.
  7. D. Ruffles — Frito-Lay, 1947. Ridges engineered for dips — 'Ruffles have ridges.'
  8. C. Cracker Jack — Frito-Lay, 1896. Caramel corn and peanuts with a prize in every box since 1912.
  9. D. Fritos — Frito-Lay, 1932. The corn chip that built Frito-Lay — and the walking taco's base.
  10. B. Ruffles — Frito-Lay, 1947. Ridges engineered for dips — 'Ruffles have ridges.'
