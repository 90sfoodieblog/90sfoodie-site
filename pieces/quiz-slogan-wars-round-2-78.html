Slogan Wars, Round 2

The Slogan Wars, Round 2 round: ten questions from the archive's files, each with its answer waiting in the key below. In Slogan Wars, Round 2 the facts were old before they were trivia — that is what makes them fair game.

  1. Which product promised: “Give me a break”?
    A. Orbitz
    B. Klondike Bar
    C. Kit Kat
    D. Urkel-O's
  2. In what year did Bagel Bites first reach shelves?
    A. 1978
    B. 1985
    C. 1990
    D. 1981
  3. Name the product in this memory: “Flash-frozen beads invented by a microbiologist in 1988; the future lasted decades at malls and ballparks.”
    A. Klondike Bar
    B. Drumstick
    C. Dippin' Dots
    D. Totino's Pizza Rolls
  4. Froot Loops came from Kellogg's. Which of these did too?
    A. Apple Jacks
    B. Burger Bundles
    C. Tootsie Roll
    D. Peanut Butter M&M's
  5. Name the product in this memory: “The rainbow's US arrival; Wild Berry joined in 1989.”
    A. Sour Patch Kids
    B. Junior Mints
    C. Good & Plenty
    D. Skittles
  6. Which product promised: “Fully Loaded”?
    A. Kit Kat
    B. Surge
    C. Slice
    D. Dunkaroos
  7. Trix came from General Mills. Which of these did too?
    A. Pringles
    B. McDLT
    C. Hidden Treasures
    D. Reptar Crunch
  8. Which debuted first — Wheaties, Sour Patch Kids, Fruity Pebbles, or Dippin' Dots?
    A. Dippin' Dots
    B. Sour Patch Kids
    C. Fruity Pebbles
    D. Wheaties
  9. Which of these can you still buy today — Cheetos, Hidden Treasures, Hydrox, or Josta?
    A. Hydrox
    B. Cheetos
    C. Josta
    D. Hidden Treasures
  10. Which of these vanished from the shelves — Butterfinger BB's, Hostess CupCakes, Lay's, or Slim Jim?
    A. Butterfinger BB's
    B. Slim Jim
    C. Hostess CupCakes
    D. Lay's

The Answer Key

  1. C. Kit Kat — Hershey, 1935. The break-apart bar with the jingle everyone can finish.
  2. B. Bagel Bites — Bagel Bites, 1985. When pizza's on a bagel, you can eat pizza any time — the jingle is the record.
  3. C. Dippin' Dots — Dippin' Dots, 1988. Flash-frozen beads invented by a microbiologist in 1988; the future lasted decades at malls and ballparks.
  4. A. Apple Jacks — Kellogg's, 1965. The ads' eternal mystery: they don't taste like apples, and the kids didn't care.
  5. D. Skittles — Mars, 1982. The rainbow's US arrival; Wild Berry joined in 1989.
  6. B. Surge — Coca-Cola, 1996–2003. Coke's citrus answer to Mountain Dew; fans campaigned it back in 2014.
  7. C. Hidden Treasures — General Mills, 1993–1995. Plain-looking squares, some hiding cherry, orange, or grape fillings inside.
  8. D. Wheaties — General Mills, 1924. The orange box put Michael Jordan on more breakfast tables than anyone.
  9. B. Cheetos — Frito-Lay, 1948. Chester Cheetah took over the ads in 1986, too cool for the original mouse.
  10. A. Butterfinger BB's — Nestlé, 1992–2006. Marble-sized Butterfingers, fronted by Bart Simpson at the height of Simpsons mania.
