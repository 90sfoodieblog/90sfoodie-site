The Answer Key

  1. A. Book It! — Pizza Hut, 1984. Pizza Hut's reading program: read books, earn a personal pan pizza — the 90s' tastiest homework.
  2. A. Haribo Goldbears — Haribo, 1922. The original gummi bears, from Bonn in 1922, in US stores by 1982.
  3. A. Boo Berry — General Mills, 1973. The blueberry ghost cereal that completed the monster trio.
  4. D. Chocodiles — Hostess, early years. The chocolate-covered Twinkie, hunted for decades by its mascot Chauncey.
  5. B. Bubble Jug — Trolli, 1990. Powder that turned to gum in your mouth, shaken from a jug.
  6. B. Gushers — Betty Crocker, 1991. Fruit snacks with a liquid center that burst — heads turned into fruit in the ads. (Filed here by lunchbox logic.)
  7. D. Twix — Mars, 1979. Two bars per pack — the sharing argument built in.
  8. B. Subway — Subway, 1965. Founded as Pete's Super Submarines in 1965; the 90s made it the 'fresh' alternative.
  9. C. Ruffles — Frito-Lay, 1947. Ridges engineered for dips — 'Ruffles have ridges.'
  10. B. Cinnamon Toast Crunch — General Mills, 1984. Cinnamon-sugar squares; Crazy Squares in the modern ads.
