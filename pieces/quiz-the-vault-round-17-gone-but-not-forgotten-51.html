The Vault, Round 17: Gone But Not Forgotten

This is The Vault, Round 17: Gone But Not Forgotten — a ten-question round built entirely from documented shelf history. The Vault, Round 17: Gone But Not Forgotten asks no trick questions and repeats no rumors: the archive only asks what it can prove. The The Vault, Round 17: Gone But Not Forgotten key is printed at the end, the archive way.

The Answer Key

  1. C. King Vitaman — Quaker, 1970–2019. The crowned king of the value shelf, poured for nearly fifty years.
  2. B. Sprinkle Spangles — General Mills, 1993–1998. A star-shaped cereal coated in candy sprinkles, wished into being by a genie.
  3. B. Dunkaroos — Betty Crocker, 1990–2012. Cookies and frosting dip — the lunchbox legend, revived in 2020 after an 8-year absence.
  4. A. Urkel-O's — Ralston, 1991–1992. A Family Matters cereal — did it do that? It did, briefly.
  5. A. Doritos 3D's — Frito-Lay, 1998–2004. Puffed 3D Doritos in a plastic canister — the decade's chip engineering marvel.
  6. C. Slice — Pepsi, 1984–2009. Pepsi's juice-blended soda line; its lemon-lime handed the torch to Sierra Mist in 1999.
  7. C. S'mores Crunch — General Mills, 1982–1988. Graham squares, chocolate, and marshmallow — returned in spirit as Smorz in 2003.
  8. C. King Vitaman — Quaker, 1970–2019. The crowned king of the value shelf, poured for nearly fifty years.
  9. A. Nintendo Cereal System — Ralston, 1988–1989. Two cereals in one box — Super Mario fruit and Zelda berry, split down the middle.
  10. A. Fruit Stripe — Fruit Stripe, 1960–2024. Tattooed stripes on every stick; the flavor was famously brief.
