The Whole Store: Grand Mix, Round 5

The Whole Store: Grand Mix, Round 5 deals in the verifiable: who made it, when it lived, what it promised on the box. Ten questions, one printed answer key, zero guesses — in The Whole Store: Grand Mix, Round 5, the record does the talking.

  1. In what year did Taco Bell Value Menu first reach shelves?
    A. 1983
    B. 1990
    C. 1988
    D. 1986
  2. Who fronted E.L. Fudge in the ads?
    A. Tony the Tiger
    B. Ernie Keebler
    C. Sugar Bear
    D. Slimer
  3. Which aisle of the 90s did Eskimo Pie call home?
    A. Frozen & Ice Cream
    B. Cereals
    C. Fast Food
    D. Snack Cakes & Cookies
  4. Which company gave us Bit-O-Honey?
    A. Tastykake
    B. Eskimo Pie
    C. Sonic
    D. Bit-O-Honey
  5. Which company gave us Chips Ahoy!?
    A. Fla-Vor-Ice
    B. Wendy's
    C. Hot Pockets
    D. Nabisco
  6. Chocodiles came from Hostess. Which of these did too?
    A. Ho Hos
    B. Flintstones Push-Up
    C. Choco Taco
    D. Reptar Crunch
  7. Which product promised: “Once you pop, the fun don't stop”?
    A. SoBe
    B. Candy Necklaces
    C. Pringles
    D. Flintstones Push-Up
  8. Which debuted first — Fudgsicle, Big League Chew, Bagel Bites, or Munchos?
    A. Bagel Bites
    B. Big League Chew
    C. Munchos
    D. Fudgsicle
  9. Which debuted first — Tootsie Roll, Peanut Butter Crunch, Sierra Mist, or SweeTarts?
    A. SweeTarts
    B. Tootsie Roll
    C. Sierra Mist
    D. Peanut Butter Crunch
  10. Which of these vanished from the shelves — Arch Deluxe, Chocodiles, Flintstones Push-Up, or Sour Patch Kids?
    A. Chocodiles
    B. Sour Patch Kids
    C. Flintstones Push-Up
    D. Arch Deluxe

The Answer Key

  1. C. Taco Bell Value Menu — Taco Bell, 1988. The 59-79-99 era made Taco Bell the broke teenager's banquet.
  2. B. E.L. Fudge — Keebler, 1986. Elf-shaped sandwich cookies named for Everybody Loves Fudge.
  3. A. Eskimo Pie — Eskimo Pie, 1921. The first chocolate-covered ice cream bar with a foil wrapper.
  4. D. Bit-O-Honey — Bit-O-Honey, 1924. Honey taffy with almond bits — the slow chew.
  5. D. Chips Ahoy! — Nabisco, 1963. The blue bag of the cookie aisle.
  6. A. Ho Hos — Hostess, 1967. The chocolate-swiss-roll pinwheel.
  7. C. Pringles — Kellogg's, 1968. Chips engineered as identical saddles, stacked in a can to never break. The slogan debuted in 1994.
  8. D. Fudgsicle — Popsicle, 1920. The fudge pop of a thousand summer afternoons.
  9. B. Tootsie Roll — Tootsie, 1896. The owl's question outlived the century.
  10. D. Arch Deluxe — McDonald's, 1996–2000. The burger for grown-ups, with a peppered bacon and a secret sauce — kids in ads were disgusted on purpose.
