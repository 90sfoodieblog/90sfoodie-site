The Answer Key

  1. C. Ghostbusters Cereal — Ralston, 1985–1989. Ghost-shaped marshmallows and Slimer on the box.
  2. B. Domino's 30-Minute Guarantee — Domino's, 1973–1993. Free or discounted if late, until safety concerns ended it in 1993.
  3. B. Mr. Pibb — Coca-Cola, 1972–2001. The Dr Pepper rival renamed Pibb Xtra in 2001.
  4. B. McLean Deluxe — McDonald's, 1991–1996. The 91%-fat-free burger bound with seaweed extract; the decade's diet-burger monument.
  5. C. Dunkaroos — Betty Crocker, 1990–2012. Cookies and frosting dip — the lunchbox legend, revived in 2020 after an 8-year absence.
  6. C. Gushers — Betty Crocker, 1991. Fruit snacks with a liquid center that burst — heads turned into fruit in the ads. (Filed here by lunchbox logic.)
  7. B. Surge — Coca-Cola, 1996–2003. Coke's citrus answer to Mountain Dew; fans campaigned it back in 2014.
  8. A. Oreo O's — Post, 1998–2007. Chocolate rings with marshmallow bits; left the US in 2007, returned a decade later.
  9. A. Quisp — Quaker, 1965–1975. The propeller-headed alien's cereal, later an internet revival darling.
  10. C. Frute Brute — General Mills, 1974–1982. The werewolf's fruit cereal, retired in the 80s and revived for Halloween seasons.
