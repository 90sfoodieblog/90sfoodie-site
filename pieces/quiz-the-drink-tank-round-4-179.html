The Drink Tank, Round 4

The Drink Tank, Round 4 deals in the verifiable: who made it, when it lived, what it promised on the box. Ten questions, one printed answer key, zero guesses — in The Drink Tank, Round 4, the record does the talking.

    A. Bug Juice
    B. Kool-Aid Bursts

    A. Yoo-hoo

The Answer Key

  1. B. Kool-Aid Bursts — Kraft, 1994. Twist-top bottles of Kool-Aid for the lunchbox run, from 1994.
  2. A. Yoo-hoo — Yoo-hoo, 1928. The chocolate drink (not milk) of ballfields and bodegas.
  3. B. Mountain Dew — Pepsi, 1948. The extreme-sports era's fuel; the 90s made it a lifestyle.
  4. D. Yoo-hoo — Yoo-hoo, 1928. The chocolate drink (not milk) of ballfields and bodegas.
  5. B. Ecto Cooler — Hi-C, 1987–2001. The green tangerine juice box with Slimer on the front, outliving the cartoon by a decade.
  6. C. Faygo — Faygo, 1907. Detroit's soda — Rock & Rye — and the Insane Clown Posse's eternal spray.
  7. D. Tampico — Tampico, 1989. Gallon citrus punch — the cookout and corner-store staple from 1989.
  8. A. Pepsi A.M. — Pepsi, 1989–1990. A higher-caffeine 'morning cola,' gone within a year.
  9. C. Slice — Pepsi, 1984–2009. Pepsi's juice-blended soda line; its lemon-lime handed the torch to Sierra Mist in 1999.
  10. D. Fruitopia — Coca-Cola, 1994–2003. Kaleidoscope-colored fruit drinks with names like Strawberry Passion Awareness.
