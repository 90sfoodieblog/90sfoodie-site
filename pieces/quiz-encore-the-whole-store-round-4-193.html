Encore: The Whole Store, Round 4

Ten questions on Encore: The Whole Store, Round 4 — every one drawn from the archive's own record, every answer checked against the shelf it came from. The Encore: The Whole Store, Round 4 key waits at the bottom; no peeking, that's the rule of the rewind.

  1. Which company gave us Arby's?
    A. Planters
    B. SoBe
    C. Arby's
    D. Little Hug
  2. What was the famous line attached to Gatorade?
    A. Breakfast of Champions
    B. Pizza in the morning, pizza in the evening, pizza at suppertime
    C. Be like Mike
    D. Milk's favorite cookie
  3. Which aisle of the 90s did Bit-O-Honey call home?
    A. Candy
    B. Frozen & Ice Cream
    C. Sodas & Drinks
    D. Snacks & Chips
  4. Name the product in this memory: “Crackers and a cheese spread with the little red plastic stick. (A lunchbox crossover king.)”
    A. Cheetos
    B. Handi-Snacks
    C. Fritos
    D. Cracker Jack
  5. Which product promised: “Keeps the hot side hot and the cool side cool”?
    A. McDLT
    B. Reese's Puffs
    C. Totino's Pizza Rolls
    D. Ring Pop
  6. Name the product in this memory: “The juice box standard; Flashin' Fruit Punch stained a generation of shirts.”
    A. Orbitz
    B. Hi-C Juice Boxes
    C. Squeezit
    D. Brisk Iced Tea
  7. Which aisle of the 90s did Pillsbury Doughboy call home?
    A. Snack Cakes & Cookies
    B. Snacks & Chips

    A. McDonald's Fried Apple Pie
    B. Bagel Bites

    B. Totino's Pizza Rolls
    C. Faygo
    D. Doritos

The Answer Key

  1. C. Arby's — Arby's, 1964. The name is R.B. — Raffel Brothers, and roast beef — not America spelled out.
  2. C. Gatorade — Pepsico, 1965. The 1991 'Be Like Mike' jingle made sports drink a Michael Jordan anthem.
  3. A. Bit-O-Honey — Bit-O-Honey, 1924. Honey taffy with almond bits — the slow chew.
  4. B. Handi-Snacks — Kraft, 1980. Crackers and a cheese spread with the little red plastic stick. (A lunchbox crossover king.)
  5. A. McDLT — McDonald's, 1984–1990. Two-chamber styrofoam packaging — Jason Alexander sang its praises in 1985.
  6. B. Hi-C Juice Boxes — Hi-C, 1955. The juice box standard; Flashin' Fruit Punch stained a generation of shirts.
  7. A. Pillsbury Doughboy — Pillsbury, 1961. Poppin' Fresh has giggled at a finger poke since 1961.
  8. D. Lunchables — Oscar Mayer, 1988. Build-your-own turkey, cheese, and crackers — the lunchbox power symbol from 1988.
  9. A. McDonald's Fried Apple Pie — McDonald's, 1968–1992. The original pie was fried until 1992, when the baked version took over in the US.
  10. A. Quisp — Quaker, 1965–1975. The propeller-headed alien's cereal, later an internet revival darling.
