Freezer & Fryer, Round 2

Freezer & Fryer, Round 2 deals in the verifiable: who made it, when it lived, what it promised on the box. Ten questions, one printed answer key, zero guesses — in Freezer & Fryer, Round 2, the record does the talking.

  1. Which product promised: “Pizza in the morning, pizza in the evening, pizza at suppertime”?
    A. Funyuns
    B. Ecto Cooler
    C. Juicy Juice
    D. Bagel Bites
  2. Drumstick came from Nestlé. Which of these did too?
    A. Nerds
    B. Otter Pops
    C. Hardee's Frisco Burger
    D. Lay's
  3. Which aisle of the 90s did Dippin' Dots call home?
    A. Snack Cakes & Cookies
    B. Cereals
    C. Frozen & Ice Cream
    D. Lunchbox
  4. In what year did Little Caesars Pizza!Pizza! first reach shelves?
    A. 1979
    B. 1954
    C. 1989
    D. 1986
  5. Which company gave us Wendy's Frosty?
    A. Wendy's
    B. Bug Juice
    C. General Foods
    D. Hawaiian Punch
  6. In what year did Hot Pockets first reach shelves?
    A. 1980
    B. 1988
    C. 1983
    D. 1958
  7. Which company gave us Totino's Pizza Rolls?
    A. Juicy Juice
    B. Totino's
    C. Cry Baby
    D. Nik-L-Nip
  8. Which debuted first — Totino's Pizza Rolls, Hot Pockets, TCBY, or Subway?
    A. Hot Pockets
    B. Subway
    C. Totino's Pizza Rolls
    D. TCBY
  9. Which of these vanished from the shelves — Burger Bundles, Mike and Ike, Tongue Splashers, or Tootsie Roll?
    A. Burger Bundles
    B. Mike and Ike
    C. Tongue Splashers
    D. Tootsie Roll
  10. Which debuted first — McDonald's Fried Apple Pie, Chicken McNuggets, Toaster Strudel, or Sonic Drive-In?
    A. McDonald's Fried Apple Pie
    B. Chicken McNuggets
    C. Sonic Drive-In
    D. Toaster Strudel

The Answer Key

  1. D. Bagel Bites — Bagel Bites, 1985. When pizza's on a bagel, you can eat pizza any time — the jingle is the record.
  2. A. Nerds — Nestlé, 1983. Tiny tangy pebbles in a two-chamber box, two flavors at war.
  3. C. Dippin' Dots — Dippin' Dots, 1988. Flash-frozen beads invented by a microbiologist in 1988; the future lasted decades at malls and ballparks.
  4. A. Little Caesars Pizza!Pizza! — Little Caesars, 1979. Two pizzas for the price of one, in the long double box on paper.
  5. A. Wendy's Frosty — Wendy's, 1969. Too thick for a straw, too thin for a spoon — fries dipped in it are the law.
  6. C. Hot Pockets — Hot Pockets, 1983. The microwaveable turnover with the crisping sleeve sleight of hand.
  7. B. Totino's Pizza Rolls — Totino's, 1968. The molten pocket squares — patience was the only safe temperature.
  8. B. Subway — Subway, 1965. Founded as Pete's Super Submarines in 1965; the 90s made it the 'fresh' alternative.
  9. A. Burger Bundles — Burger King, 1987–1990. Mini burgers sold in six-packs — the slider era before sliders were cool.
  10. C. Sonic Drive-In — Sonic, 1953. Carhops on skates and a happy hour of half-price drinks.
