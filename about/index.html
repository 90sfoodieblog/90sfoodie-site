About 90s Foodie

90s snacks, drinks & fast food throwbacks 🍿 By Denny

90s Foodie is one fan's field guide to the snacks, drinks, and fast-food throwbacks of the 1990s — the glass bottles, the cereal-aisle legends, the limited runs that vanished before we could say goodbye. The blog on Substack tells the long stories; this archive is the memory palace they live in.

How an entry is built

Every entry starts as a long story on the blog — a deep dive into one snack's rise, reign, and disappearance, researched from period packaging and advertising, the maker's own history, and the memories readers send in. The archive page is where that story lives permanently, filed next to the hard facts: who made it, the years it spent on shelves, its flavors and variants, and its fate. When the record and nostalgia disagree, the page goes with the record — and says so.

Three fates, tracked honestly

Every snack here carries one of three verdicts, checked rather than assumed. Still sold means you can buy it today, usually in modern dress — its classic-era packaging is what the archive preserves. Brought back means a revival actually happened, and the page names the year it returned. Discontinued means gone from shelves — and for the true icons, the page adds a vintage hunt, because sealed boxes and original packaging still turn up from collectors and marketplace sellers.

The shelves

The archive is filed on six shelves — Drinks, Candy, Cereals, Chips & Snacks, Fast Food, and Frozen Treats — and it grows one post at a time. A Snack of the Day rotates through the collection on the homepage, readers rate their own memories with the Remember it? stars on every page, and the weekly bracket pits the legends against each other. If a favorite of yours is missing, it has not been forgotten. It just has not had its post yet.

New entries arrive with every post. The community gathers on Reddit at u/90sFoodieblog today — and at r/90sFoodie when the doors open in November 2026.

90s Foodie is written and kept by Denny — one fan with a long memory and a working search engine. Say hello, send a correction, or nominate the snack that got away: reader suggestions genuinely run the roadmap.
